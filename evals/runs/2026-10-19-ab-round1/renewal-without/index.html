Four things, for every contract, always visible

  • Owner

    One named person is responsible for each renewal. Not a shared inbox, not “legal.”

  • Notice deadline

    The renewal date minus the notice period, worked out for you. That is the date that actually matters.

  • Next decision

    Written as a question someone can answer: renew, renegotiate, reduce, or let it end.

  • Review trail

    Who looked at what, and when. So the next renewal starts from a record instead of memory.

How a renewal moves through Ledgerlight

  1. 1

    Record the terms that drive the date

    Renewal or end date, notice period, and whether it renews on its own. The notice deadline follows from those.

  2. 2

    Name an owner and the next decision

    The owner sees the deadline coming and knows what they are being asked to decide, well before the window closes.

  3. 3

    Keep the trail as you go

    Each review, request and decision is noted with a date and a name, so the reasoning is still there next year.

Questions worth asking first

Who is Ledgerlight for?

Small legal teams that own a company's contracts, often one to a handful of people who also do everything else. If you are outside counsel tracking renewals on behalf of clients, we would like to hear how you do it today; mention it in your request.

Is the example on this page the product?

It is a working sketch of the core idea: owner, notice deadline, next decision and review trail. It runs in your browser on sample data and stores nothing anywhere else. The consultation is where we look at your actual contracts and workflow.

What security or compliance commitments can you make?

We are not going to list certifications here. Bring your security and data-handling questions to the consultation and we will answer them directly, including anything we cannot yet commit to.

What should I bring to a consultation?

A rough count of the contracts you track, how you track them now (spreadsheet, calendar reminders, a contract system, memory), and one renewal that went worse than it should have.
