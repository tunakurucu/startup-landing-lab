How a pool runs

Three moments, in order. The same gift tag is the thing everyone looks at; the money question never turns into a group-chat thread.

  1. 1

    Someone starts the gift

    The organizer names who it’s for, what the present is and what it costs. Nobody has to pay it all upfront.

    For Maya · turning 30 Ceramic pour-over set $0 in · $180 to go
  2. 2

    Friends add what they can

    One link goes into the group chat. Each friend opens it, adds an amount that works for them and leaves a line for the card.

    Jonah, in the “Maya’s 30th” group chat Going in on the pour-over set for Maya. Add what you can, it’s $180 total. poolday.example/maya-30
  3. 3

    The organizer sees what’s left

    The tag always shows how much is in and how much is still needed, so the organizer knows whether to nudge, top up, or just go and buy it.

    For Maya · 5 friends in $140 in · $40 still needed

The link and message above are illustrative; poolday.example is a reserved example address, not a live page.

What’s not decided yet

Poolday hasn’t run a real pool. There are no reviews to show and no totals to report, so this page doesn’t pretend otherwise. A few things are still open, and they matter:

  • How the money moves. Who holds it, when it’s paid out and to whom isn’t settled, which is why no example here asks for a card.
  • Who sees which amounts. The example shows everyone’s amount on the tag. Whether friends see each other’s, or only the organizer does, is undecided.
  • Fees. Not set. There is no pricing on this page because there isn’t any yet.

One present. Everyone who wants to be in on it, is.
