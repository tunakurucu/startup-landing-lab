Heat water when power is cheap.
Have it hot when you need it.

Tankshift is a controller your electrician adds to the electric water heater you already have. It learns when the house uses hot water and does the heating in the cheapest hours of your tariff, so the tank is hot at seven in the morning and the bill is not.

How it works

Nothing changes about your hot water

The tank, the plumbing and the taps stay as they are. Tankshift sits on the electrical side, between the circuit and the heater, and decides when the element runs. The heater's own thermostat and safety cut-out stay in charge of temperature.

It learns your mornings

A tank of hot water keeps for hours. Tankshift watches when the house actually draws hot water, then heats far enough ahead that the tank is ready, using the cheapest hours it can find before each use.

It follows your tariff, not a guess

Your electrician enters the peak and off-peak hours from your tariff, or the controller takes them from a utility programme. If the tariff changes, the schedule changes with it.

About the savings

How much this is worth depends on the gap between your peak and off-peak prices and on how much hot water you use. Those differ from house to house, so we don't print a number we can't stand behind. The strip above shows the shape of the change; your bill shows the size.

For electricians

You decide whether this goes in. Here is what the install involves, as it stands today.

Fits
Electric storage water heaters, single or dual element, on their existing circuit.
Wiring
In line between the supply and the heater. The heater's thermostat and high-limit stay in circuit; Tankshift only decides whether power reaches them.
Plumbing
None. No mixing valve, no new tank.
Mounting
Beside the heater, near the existing disconnect.
Setup
Enter the tariff hours on the unit. No app for the homeowner, no account to create on site.
Override
A boost control on the unit heats now, for the odd day with visitors.

For utility programmes

Water heating is one of the largest loads in an all-electric home, and one of the few that can move several hours without anyone noticing.

Shifting you can count

Each unit records its heating hours by tariff band. A programme gets the per-household figures it needs to pay a rebate on what was actually shifted, not on what was modelled.

Event days

Tankshift can take a signal to hold heating through a peak event, within the limit of keeping the household in hot water. The household's usage pattern sets that limit, not a fixed duration.

One pilot, running now

One utility is piloting Tankshift in customer homes. We can't name them yet. When the pilot reports, the figures go here, with the tariff and the usage behind them.
