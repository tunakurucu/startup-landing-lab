# DESIGN-RECORD

Page: `index.html` (static, opens from disk). Mode: Build. Skill: startup-landing-design v0.4. Date: 2026-10-20.

## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: Three audiences in one page: homeowners on time-of-use tariffs (care about the bill), electricians (decide whether it gets installed, fear callbacks) and utility rebate programmes (pay for shifted load, need measurement). Category: a controller for electric tank water heaters. Model: hardware plus install, priced per region with rebates; no subscription planned (brief gives no price). No brand files supplied; name "Tankshift" only.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): For a household on a time-of-use tariff, the water heater stops heating at peak price and heats in the cheap hours instead, because an electrician wires a controller inline on the heater's feed that fills the tank before each peak block, holds through it, and tops up only if the tank runs low.
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: Doing nothing (the thermostat heats whenever the tank cools, including at peak price); a mechanical timer on the heater circuit (runs on the clock, not the tariff, and can leave the tank cold); replacing the heater with a heat-pump unit that has its own schedule (expensive, a different purchase).
- The one advantage only this product has: It is added to the heater the household already owns, by an electrician in one visit, and it trades price against hot water with an explicit reserve rule (hold through the peak, but heat at peak price if the tank runs low, and log it). A timer cannot do the reserve; a replacement heater is not an add-on.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: Existing noun, "a controller an electrician adds to your electric water heater" (subhead, first screen). Costs one sentence; the example day teaches the rest.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: Best fit: a resistive electric tank heater, a tariff with cheap hours or a utility programme paying for shifted load, and an electrician who can reach the feed. Not for gas, tankless or heat-pump heaters, or flat tariffs with no cheap hours (stated as a checklist beside the form).
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet. The brief says there are no customer stories; the hero adapts the client's own line from the brief ("water heaters that wait for cheap power") instead.
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: Commitment and spam, plus "what does joining even do when the region isn't public?" Hero line: "Free, no commitment. Installs start in one region; the list opens with it." Form line: "Free, no commitment. This form is not connected to a server yet: it keeps your details on this device only and sends nothing until the list opens."
- Conversion action and destination: "Join the install list" (hero and header buttons link to `#join`; the form's submit button carries the same label). The form validates locally, stores the entry in localStorage and says plainly that nothing is sent. Endpoint to supply by the client: the install-list form action (see Remaining weaknesses).
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Will I run out of hot water during the peak?" → the example-day chart's tank-level line with the reserve rule, and the "Draws that ran short" row; the Evening showers and Night rate presets show the hold giving way to a peak-price top-up, and one preset (night rate + evening showers) shows a draw running short.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "How much does it save?" → the readout table shows kWh heated at peak, shoulder and off-peak for thermostat-alone versus Tankshift, labelled as a model and not a bill forecast; no currency figure is printed anywhere (brief: no number we can't back up).
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: Electrician: "How does it wire in, and is it listed?" → the install sketch (desktop and a stacked phone version) with four steps, and the safety-listing status beside the sketch. Utility: "Can we measure it?" → the example log table, labelled illustrative, with "no remote control of the household" stated beside it.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: Functional entrance. The brief asks for a local example a visitor can try; the product's value is a visible before/after on one day; the first screen can be input → output with the install list as the single action. Immersive editorial was rejected: no footage or render exists, and hardware "in a scene" would be invented.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: Display: a condensed grotesque (TeX Gyre Heros Cn, falling back to Helvetica Neue Condensed, Arial Narrow, Roboto Condensed, then a plain sans), weight 700, tracking 0: the register of a heater nameplate and a breaker-panel label, and rare on startup pages. Body: Charter (Bitstream Charter / Charter on macOS, Georgia fallback), a serif drawn for low-resolution printing, the register of an installer sheet and a utility bill. Data: the system mono stack, tabular figures, only in the chart, the log table and figure tags. System stacks only; no font files are shipped and no network is used.
- Composition and grid: 1180px wrap, 12-column mental grid. Hero: headline on 7 columns, subhead and action on 5, aligned to the bottom; then the example panel across the full width with the chart on 7 columns and the readouts on 5. Sections alternate a 7/5 head with a 6/6 body (sketch + steps; log + points), a 3-column numbered rule list, a definition list, and a 6/6 form. Density: open promise → active example → dense rules → technical install → quiet utility log → short price → short action.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: A cool off-white (#f3f4f2), the painted steel and enamel of a utility room, with white panels ruled by 1px hairlines and no shadows. Accent: cobalt (#1646c8) for the action and for "heater on with Tankshift". Tariff tiers carry their own colours (pale blue off-peak, grey shoulder, amber peak); amber with a dark outline marks a peak-price top-up, the one exception state. The cream-and-serif default was avoided: the ground is cool, the display is sans.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: "Fig. 1 · Example only": a 24-hour strip with tariff tiers, hot-water draws, heater-on bars for thermostat-alone and for Tankshift, and a tank-level line with the reserve marked. Two radio groups (three tariffs, three households) recompute a 15-minute model in the browser; readouts fill a table (kWh by tier, top-ups, draws that ran short) and a status line announces the change. The model shows its failure modes: peak-price top-ups and, in one preset, a draw that runs short.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: Header → hero (promise, action, example day) → three rules and the override → for electricians (sketch, steps, listing status) → for rebate programmes (example log, three points, pilot status) → price → install list (fit checklist, form) → footer. Omitted on purpose: a "savings" section with a dollar figure or percentage, because the brief says it cannot be backed up; and any testimonial, logo or "trusted by" slot.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: Selecting a tariff or household redraws the chart and the readouts at once; the chart container dips to 60% opacity and back over 160ms (no-preference only) to mark the swap. The form validates and shows a status. No scroll-driven, auto-advancing or hover-only motion.

## Structural revision
- Weakest explanation or most template-like decision: In the first build the example panel stacked chart → legend → status → readout table → a large "100%" stat tile. The numbers that answer "where does the heating go?" sat about 1,100px below the headline, and the oversized percentage read as a savings claim despite its caption, the most marketing-tile-like thing on the page.
- Change made (structure, not styling): The panel body became a 7/5 grid: chart, legend, draw list and status on the left; the readout table on the right, with the moved-share rendered as one sentence ("65% of the heating that would have run at peak price now runs in cheaper hours, in this example") at 1.75rem instead of a 4rem tile. At 1440×900 the first screen now shows input, chart and the kWh table together. Hour labels switch to 3-hour "HHh" marks when the plot is under 760px so they do not collide; the chart's compact (phone) layout now begins under 560px. A second, stacked wiring sketch was added for phones in the same pass, after the screenshot gate caught the shrunk one.
