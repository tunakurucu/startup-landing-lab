## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: Software vendors that receive enterprise security questionnaires; the two people who touch them are the sales engineer (wants speed) and the security lead (approves every answer). Category: an AI agent for security questionnaires. Business model: pilots by email, no public pricing. No brand files supplied; name "Paperwren" only.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): For vendors answering security questionnaires, each answer arrives already drafted and already cited to one of their own policy sections (or flagged when no policy covers it), and the security lead approves every line before anything leaves.
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: Answering by hand from old questionnaires and a shared answer spreadsheet, pinging security in chat to confirm; or a questionnaire-automation tool that autofills from a knowledge base (Vanta Questionnaire Automation, Conveyor, Loopio); or doing nothing and losing the deal to slowness.
- The one advantage only this product has: Every drafted answer is tied to a section-level passage in the vendor's own policies, and a question the library does not cover is flagged with the closest miss instead of answered, so the approver reads two short passages rather than re-deriving the answer. (A design goal; no accuracy numbers exist yet, and the page says so.)
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: "AI agent for security questionnaires" (X for Y). Costs nothing to teach: both roles know the questionnaire; the only new idea is "cited or flagged", which the sample shows in one screen.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: Best fit: B2B software vendors with written security policies and enough enterprise deals that questionnaires recur, where a named security lead must approve answers. Not for: companies whose policies live in people's heads (a thin library gives a thin draft and a long flag list); the page says this in "Not for you yet".
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet. The brief supplies no interviews or tickets; the hero adapts the brief's own wording ("cites a source for each answer or flags it for review", "security lead approves every answer").
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: Data access and a sales call (a vendor's policies are sensitive; "request a pilot" sounds like a demo funnel). Line beside the button: "Opens a blank email to pilots@paperwren.example. Nothing to upload, no call to book."
- Conversion action and destination: "Request a pilot by email" → `mailto:pilots@paperwren.example?subject=Pilot request`, used identically in the header, hero, pilot section and footer.
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Does the agent make things up?" → the sample card: Q 4.3 drafted with two section-level citations whose policy excerpts are shown in full beside the answer; Q 9.1 with no covering policy is flagged and nothing is drafted, with the closest miss named and rejected.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "Will this bypass my security lead?" → the review strip in the card (Awaiting approval → Approve / Send back, with a local status change) and the four-stage handoff rail naming the owner at each stage.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "Is this real, and what does a pilot cost me?" → the pilot section: a four-step pilot that re-drafts a questionnaire they already answered, a fact box that prints "Not measured yet" for accuracy and time saved, three unnamed design partners with a note, and the pricing note; plus the Notes section where every claim on the page carries its source or its flag.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: Workflow theater. The value unfolds through states (read → search → match → draft or flag → approve); one work item (question 4.3 / 9.1) moves through them with an owner and a next action at each stage.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: Display and body: a Helvetica/Arial system stack (`"Helvetica Neue", "TeX Gyre Heros", "Liberation Sans", Helvetica, Arial`), the face questionnaires and policy forms are actually set in; bold 700 for the promise, 400 for text. Quoted policy passages in a text serif (Charter / Iowan / Georgia) so a source reads as a document, not as UI. Mono (SF Mono / Menlo / DejaVu Sans Mono) for question IDs, section numbers, status chips and note marks, where alignment and "this is an identifier" matter. No web fonts: the page must open from disk with no network, so every role is a system stack and rendering differs by machine (recorded under Remaining weaknesses).
- Composition and grid: 1280px max width; hero is an 11/13 split at ≥1080px (promise left, sample card right), stacked below. Steps are a ruled list with a 3.5rem number column; the handoff rail is four equal cells with a hairline grid; the pilot section is 7/5 (steps and action / fact box); notes are a 64ch ruled list.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: White paper ground (the questionnaire is a document), hairline rules in warm grey, a very light "desk" tint only for the agent log, source excerpts and the fact box. Accent colours mark state and action only: ink fill for the one action; blue for citations and note marks; amber for flags; green for approved.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: The sample answer card: two real tabs (Q 4.3 cited, Q 9.1 flagged), an agent log, the draft with inline [1][2] marks, the cited passages in full with review dates, and a review strip whose Approve / Send back / Write the answer / Mark not applicable buttons change a `role=status` line locally and offer "Reset the sample". "Replay the draft" hides the finished state and brings each step back in order; the finished state is what first paint shows.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: Hero (promise + sample) → How it works (four ordered steps, with where it stops) → Who it's for (handoff rail with owners, then one paragraph per role, then "Not for you yet") → The pilot (what a pilot is, the fact box with "Not measured yet" flags, pricing note, final action) → Notes (each claim's source or flag) → footer. Omitted on purpose: a logo strip / testimonial slot (nothing supplied; the three partners are unnamed) and a generic feature-card row.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: Tabs and review buttons change the artifact's state immediately. Replay is the only sequence; it exists to show the order (search before draft, draft only after a match). Motion is confined to a 180–220ms fade/4px rise per log line inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; under reduce the lines simply appear in order. Nothing moves on its own.

## Two hero variants before code
- Axis the variants differ on (structure, density, emphasis, type or voice): Structure: split (promise beside the card) versus stacked (wide headline, then a full-width questionnaire sheet with several rows).
- Category default and anti-default this plan avoids: Default for security/compliance SaaS: dark navy or white ground, Inter, a shield or lock illustration, a "Trusted by" logo row and SOC 2 / ISO badges, "AI-powered questionnaire automation" headline, a framed dashboard screenshot under a centred h1. Anti-default: cream ground, editorial serif display, a mascot and wry voice (what Vanta does). This plan: white document ground, a bold Helvetica/Arial-stack headline, no badges, no mascot, and the real artifact (one answer with its sources and its approval) instead of a dashboard.
