It drafts only from your policy library, and says where each line came from

Four steps, in this order, for every question. The order is the point: a draft exists only after a matching passage does.

  1. 1

    You load your policies

    Security policies, standards and procedures, as they are today. The library is the only thing the agent reads when it answers. No web search, no filling gaps from general knowledge.1

  2. 2

    It finds the passages that answer each question

    Matches are made at the section level, not the document level, so a reviewer can read the exact lines an answer rests on.

  3. 3

    It drafts, or it flags

    A draft carries a citation for each claim it makes. If no passage covers the question, nothing is drafted. The question is flagged with the closest miss, so a person can answer it or mark it not applicable.

  4. 4

    Your security lead approves, edits or sends back

    Every answer waits for approval. Nothing reaches the buyer before that, and the approval sits next to the answer and its sources.

Sales engineers get the draft back. The security lead keeps the last word.

Two people touch every questionnaire and want different things from it. The handoff between them is the product; here is who holds the work at each stage.

  1. Owner Sales engineer

    Questionnaire arrives

    Hands the buyer's questionnaire in as it came, and gets on with the deal.

  2. Owner Paperwren

    Every question drafted or flagged

    Cited answers in one list, flagged questions in another, each flag with its reason.

  3. Owner Security lead

    Approve, edit or send back

    Reads the answer beside its source. Flagged questions reach her first, not last.

  4. Owner Sales engineer

    Approved set goes to the buyer

    Only lines the security lead approved. The approval record stays with each answer.

If you're the sales engineer

You send the questionnaire in. You get back a draft where every answer already says where it came from, plus a short list of flagged questions that need a person. Chasing answers in chat becomes reviewing a list.5

If you're the security lead

Nothing is sent in your name without your approval. Each answer shows the policy section it rests on, so checking it means reading two short passages, not working the answer out again. Flagged questions reach you with the reason.

Not for you yet if your policies live in people's heads. The agent can only cite what is written down, so a thin library produces a thin draft and a long flag list.

The pilot: your last questionnaire, drafted again

We have no accuracy or time-saved numbers to show you. A pilot produces them on your own questionnaire, where they mean something.

  1. Pick a questionnaire you have already answered, and the policies behind it.
  2. Paperwren drafts it from those policies alone.
  3. Your security lead compares the two, line by line: how many answers were cited, how many flagged, how many she would have changed.
  4. Those are your numbers. Keep them whatever you decide.
