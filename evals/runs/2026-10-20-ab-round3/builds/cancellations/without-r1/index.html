How a slot gets filled

Four steps. Two of them are yours, and they take seconds.

  1. 1

    Your front desk builds a waitlist

    Patients who'd take an earlier time give their name, mobile number and the days that work. They agree to texts when they join, and can leave any time by replying STOP.

  2. 2

    A patient cancels

    Someone at the desk marks the slot open in Chairtime. That's the whole job on your side: a time, a tap.

  3. 3

    Chairtime texts the waitlist, one patient at a time

    Each patient gets a few minutes to reply. The first YES takes the slot. Anyone who was texted and didn't get it is told it's gone, and keeps their place in line. Nobody further down the list hears a thing.

  4. 4

    You book them as usual

    Chairtime tells the desk who took it. You put them in your schedule the way you always do. Nothing to integrate, nothing to migrate.

You decide who gets texted, and when

Every clinic runs its waitlist a little differently. These are set once, on the setup call, and changed whenever you like.

Order
First come, first texted, or put patients who are already due this week at the front of the line.
Reply window
How long each patient has before Chairtime moves to the next one. Most clinics pick something between five and fifteen minutes.
Minimum notice
Don't offer a slot that starts in less than, say, 45 minutes. Nobody can get there in time, and the text just annoys them.
Quiet hours
No texts before 8 am or after 7 pm. A slot that opens overnight waits until morning.
Per-patient limit
Cap how often one person can be offered a slot, so a long waitlist never turns into a nuisance for the people at the top of it.

Got a text from Chairtime?

It's real, and it came from your clinic. You asked to be told when an earlier appointment opened up, and one just did.

What to do

  • Reply YES to take the slot. You'll get a confirmation with the date, time and address.
  • Reply NO to pass. You keep your place on the waitlist.
  • Reply STOP to leave the waitlist. You won't be texted again.

How to know it's us

Every text from Chairtime names your clinic, gives one date and one time, and asks for a one-word reply. That's all it ever does.

A Chairtime text will never ask for a card number, insurance details or a password, and it will never ask you to tap a link to "confirm" anything. It won't mention why you're being seen. If a text does any of those things, it isn't from us.

Still not sure? Call your clinic on the number you already have. They can see which slots have been offered to you.

Where we stand on HIPAA

Chairtime handles patient names and phone numbers, so this isn't a footnote. We're partway through a formal HIPAA risk assessment, and until it's finished we won't describe ourselves as HIPAA compliant on this page or anywhere else.

What we can say now: a text carries a clinic name, a date, a time and an address. It never carries a diagnosis, a treatment or a clinical note. Chairtime keeps only what's needed to send that text and nothing more. Ask about the assessment on the setup call and we'll tell you exactly where it stands.

Questions clinics ask

Do patients need an app?
No. A phone that can receive texts. They reply YES or NO like they would to anyone.
Does it connect to my scheduling software?
Not today. Chairtime runs beside it: marking a slot open is a tap, and booking the patient who said yes is your normal routine.
What if nobody says yes?
The slot stays open and the desk knows it, within the window you set. You're exactly where you'd have been without Chairtime, minus the phone calls.
Who do the texts come from?
Chairtime sends them on your clinic's behalf, with your clinic's name in every message, so patients recognise who's asking.
What does it cost?
We go through pricing on the setup call, once we've seen how many cancellations you're actually dealing with each week.
