Page: `index.html` (single file, opens from disk; system font stacks, no network assets). Mode: Build. Skill: startup-landing-design v0.4.

## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: Buyers are owners and front-desk staff of small physical therapy clinics; a second audience is patients who received a Chairtime text and want to confirm it is legitimate. Category: a cancellation-filling service (texts the waitlist until a cancelled slot is taken). Business model: unknown (no pricing supplied); cost is disclosed on the setup call. Brand: name only ("Chairtime"); no logo, colours or type supplied, so the page defines them. Constraints from the brief: no HIPAA-compliance claim, no real calendar or patients in the demo, no logos, testimonials or fill-rate numbers, no scheduling link yet.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): Small PT clinics / a last-minute cancellation becomes a booked slot without the front desk working the phone / Chairtime texts the waitlist one person at a time until someone says YES, then tells the front desk who is coming.
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: Doing nothing (the chair sits empty) or the front desk calling down a paper or EMR waitlist between patients; some practice-management tools have a waitlist feature that still needs a person to call.
- The one advantage only this product has: The front desk's only job is to mark the cancellation; the offering, waiting, moving to the next person and stopping are done for them, with no double booking (one offer at a time). Inference from the brief; the one-at-a-time rule and the reply window are the plausible mechanism and are listed below for the client to confirm.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: Subcategory of scheduling: "a cancellation-filling service for small physical therapy clinics". It costs one sentence in the subhead, paid immediately.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: Best fit: a one- to three-therapist clinic with a real waitlist and a front desk that today phones people when a slot opens. Not for: large multi-site groups with an integrated EMR waitlist, clinics with no waitlist to text, or anyone who needs a signed HIPAA compliance statement today.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet; the brief supplied no interviews, tickets or reviews. The hero adapts the brief's own phrasing ("fills last-minute cancellations", "texting waitlisted patients until someone takes the slot").
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: A sales call. The line beside the button: "A working session, not a pitch: we load your waitlist with you and send a test text to your phone." (The client should confirm this is what the call does.)
- Conversion action and destination: "Book a 15-minute setup call". No scheduling tool exists yet, so every CTA points to `#setup-call`, the section that describes the call and states plainly that the calendar link is being connected. The client must replace the three `href="#setup-call"` on `.btn` elements with the scheduler URL.
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "What actually happens when someone cancels, and will it spam or double-book my patients?" → the operable fill artifact in the hero (`#how`): a sample Tuesday schedule, four selectable steps, the text thread as patients see it and the front-desk notice; plus the rules section with the three annotated message templates.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "What does my front desk have to do, and what do I have to install?" → the three-step section (`#desk`) with the sample waitlist row and the front-desk notice; the setup-call agenda.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "Is patient data safe / is this text legitimate?" → "What a text contains, and what Chairtime keeps" (what a text holds, what is stored, honest HIPAA status), and the patient section (`#patients`) with the three checks and the reply words.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: Workflow theater. The value is a sequence (cancel → offer → reply → filled) with visible artifacts (the slot, the texts, the front-desk notice), and the buyer's doubts are operational (double booking, what the desk does, what patients see).
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: Display and prose: a Charter-first serif stack (`Charter, "Iowan Old Style", "TeX Gyre Schola", Caladea, Cambria, Georgia, serif`), chosen because patients arriving from a text need the page to read like a real practice's stationery, not a growth-SaaS template, and because a text serif at 18px handles the longest sentences on the page. Product UI (schedule strip, steps, texts, labels): a Helvetica-first sans stack so the interface reads as interface. Data (clock times, step times, reply words): `ui-monospace` stack with tabular figures. No web fonts: the page must open from disk with no network, so every face is a system stack; renders here used TeX Gyre Schola and TeX Gyre Heros.
- Composition and grid: Max width 1320px. Hero: 7/5 grid (headline and subhead left, action block right, aligned to the bottom of the headline), then the full-width stage. Stage: five-cell schedule strip across the top, below it a 19rem rail (heading, four step buttons, Replay, status line) beside the text thread. Sections alternate a 5/7 grid (argument left, evidence right). Phone: one column; strip shows three cells; steps become a 2×2 grid with the time above the title.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: Warm paper `#f7f6f3` (not cream by the detector's measure: b=243) chosen for a page read at a front desk and on a patient's phone; white `#ffffff` only for the stage and evidence cards so the product surfaces sit on the page. Ink `#1b1b19` for the action. Colour otherwise marks state only: rust for cancelled, amber for offering, green for filled and for patient replies (sent bubbles). Hairline rules and overlap do the grouping; no shadows except the 2px inset state stripe on the front-desk notice.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: "The fill": a sample Tuesday with the 2:30 slot, four step buttons (1:12 cancelled, 1:14 first text, 1:19 passed and next, 1:31 filled). Clicking a step sets the slot cell's name and state badge, the clock, the visible messages (future messages collapse), a per-step "waiting" line, and a `role=status` explanation. "Replay the fill" steps 1→4 at 900 ms intervals (about 2.7 s, under 5 s); any step click wins and stops it; the button toggles to "Stop the replay". Default state on load is step 4, so captures, crawlers and no-JS visitors see the complete story. Labelled right beside it: "sample clinic with made-up patients; the times are an example, not a measured result."
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: Hero (promise, action, the fill) → the texting rules with three annotated templates and the data/HIPAA block → the front desk's three steps → for patients who got a text → the setup call with agenda, cost and the booking panel → footer. Omitted on purpose: a feature-card row, a testimonial/logo slot (nothing real exists), and an FAQ accordion (its answers are already inside the sections).
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: Step selection (state and causality). Motion: new message groups fade and rise 4px over 180 ms ease-out when a step reveals them; under reduced motion a 160 ms opacity-only fade; colour transitions on buttons 120–150 ms. Nothing moves by itself; no scroll reveals, no parallax.

## Structural revision
- Weakest explanation or most template-like decision: In v1 the first screen at 1440 was a left headline, a right-hand button and nothing of the product until 790px down; a separate h2 band then restated the h1 ("A 2:30 cancelled at 1:12 is booked again by 1:31") before the stage. A promise-plus-CTA hero over a framed panel below the fold is the most common B2B shape, and the band repeated a claim (PS-05). On phones, rewinding the steps also left a tall blank panel where hidden messages reserved space.
- Change made (structure, not styling): The stage moved into the hero: the headline block was tightened (less top padding), the h2 band was removed, and the artifact's own rail now carries a short h2 ("Cancelled at 1:12, filled by 1:31.") and the sample label, so the schedule strip, the rail heading and the first message sit inside the first screen at 1440 and the strip header at 390. Future messages now collapse instead of reserving space, and each step got a "waiting" line (reply window closes at 1:29 / 1:34) so a rewound thread explains the pause instead of showing a void. The reply window became 15 minutes so Marcus's 1:31 YES falls inside it.
