## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: BSA officers at community banks and credit unions use it; the CEO and board approve it. Category: a case-file tool for alert reviews. Business model: per-institution pricing, not public yet; a hand-built sandbox is the entry. Brand constraints from the brief: no claim that it makes a bank compliant, no "regulator-approved", no SOC 2 report yet; no brand files supplied, so type and colour are new.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): BSA officers / every monitoring alert ends as one record an examiner can follow without the officer explaining it / each alert gets a reviewer, the evidence checked, a decision and the reason, with names and times, in one place.
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: notes inside the monitoring system's alert screen plus a spreadsheet, emails and a shared folder, rebuilt into a narrative the week before the exam; or doing nothing beyond the monitoring system's own close reason (inference from the brief, not stated by the client).
- The one advantage only this product has: the reviewer, the evidence checked, the decision and the reason are one record per alert, written in the shape an examiner reads (the brief's own words); the alternative scatters them across tools.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: subcategory, "case-file tool for alert reviews" (an "X for Y" frame); costs one subhead sentence because "case file" and "alert" are the audience's own nouns.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: best fit is a community bank or credit union with one BSA officer and a few reviewers working alerts from a monitoring system. Not for large banks with a case-management module already in their monitoring platform, and not for anyone looking for detection: Casefold does not raise alerts.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet; the brief supplies no interviews, tickets or reviews. The hero adapts the brief's own phrase "a case file an examiner can follow".
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: fear that asking for a sandbox starts a sales process and touches bank data before the board has approved anything. Line beside the button: "One email to sandbox@casefold.example. We set the sandbox up by hand and reply with a login. It runs on sample alerts, so none of your bank's data is involved."
- Conversion action and destination: "Request a sandbox" → `mailto:sandbox@casefold.example?subject=Sandbox request` (the brief's address; the sandbox is set up by hand after the email). Secondary: "Step through the sample case" → `#sample`.
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Could an examiner actually follow this?" → the sample case file in the hero, stepped through five states to the examiner's view (history first, each part on one line, opens to detail), plus the "who, what, when, why" table in `#record` that points at each field in the sample.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: workflow theater. The brief asks for a sample case file a visitor can step through, and the product's value is a sequence of states on one work item (alert → reviewer → evidence → decision → examiner's view) with an owner and a next action at each state.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: display and claims in Charter (local stack: Charter / Bitstream Charter / Iowan Old Style / Georgia), a text serif drawn for documents, because the product's output is a filed record; body and sample UI in a plain Helvetica/Arial stack so the record looks like software, not a brochure; identifiers, amounts and timestamps in a system mono stack. No web fonts: the page must open offline, so every face is a system stack and the record lists it as a choice.
- Composition and grid: 1240px max; hero split 5/7 with the copy column sticky at desktop so the primary action stays beside the record while it grows; stepper (5 tabs) over a note-and-pager row over the record; below, a full-width table, a three-node flow, a 1/1.6 split board brief, and a dark closing band with the same label. Density: open promise → dense record → quiet table → plain flow → dense brief → short action.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: white page with a cool grey "desk" panel behind the white record, so the case file reads as paper on a desk (where the product is used); one accent, deep green `#0f5c4e`, for actions, the selected step and ticked evidence; amber for open status, green for decided; hairline rules and square-ish corners instead of shadows.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the sample case file ALT-2031. Five tab-buttons (ARIA tablist, arrow keys, Back/Next) change the status chip, owner, next action, the explanatory note (aria-live) and which parts of the record are shown; earlier parts fold to a one-line summary with a Show/Hide button; at step 5 the History moves to the top and every part folds, so the examiner's view is one page. Labelled "Fictional customer, staff and amounts. Illustrative only." beside the title. Without scripts the full record shows at once.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero with the artifact → the record answers who/what/when/why → where it fits (monitoring system → Casefold → SAR process) → board brief (what it is, is not, who writes, security status, cost, sandbox) → final action → footer. Deliberately omitted: a feature-card row and any "why us"/trust-logo section; there are no customers, metrics or testimonials to show, so nothing stands in for them.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: clicking a step. Motion is limited to a 900ms background highlight on the part that just appeared (marks what changed) and 160ms colour transitions on buttons and tabs; text swaps immediately; nothing moves or scrolls on its own.

## Structural revision
- Weakest explanation or most template-like decision: the record only grew. A secondary weakness: dead space under the hero copy at 1440 while the record ran past the fold (shots/before/1440-top.png).
- Change made (structure, not styling): the record now recomposes per step. Parts before the current step fold to their heading plus a one-line summary with a Show/Hide button, so the current part sits directly under the controls at every width; at step 5 the History section moves to the top of the record and all four parts fold, so the examiner's view is one page with each line opening to its detail. The hero copy column became sticky at desktop so the primary action stays beside the record as it is stepped (the dead space now has a job).
