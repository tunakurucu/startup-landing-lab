# DESIGN-RECORD

Page: `index.html` (plus `sample-agenda-item.html`, the stand-in for the original agenda item). Built from `BRIEF.md` on 2026-10-20 with the startup-landing-design skill v0.4. Opened and checked from disk.

## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: residents of one pilot city first, with city clerks and local funders reading over their shoulder; a plain-language council-agenda digest with per-neighbourhood email alerts; a small nonprofit, free to residents, no partner cities, funders or usage numbers; must read as neutral and never take a side on a council decision.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): residents who will not read a council packet / learn what the council is about to decide on their street before the meeting, in a few plain sentences / a model drafts each summary, a volunteer checks and signs it, each links to the original item, and one email goes out per meeting to the neighbourhood it names.
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: the city's own agenda packet (long PDF, legal wording, no neighbourhood filter), a neighbour's text message after the vote, or doing nothing and finding out when the work starts.
- The one advantage only this product has: a volunteer-checked plain-language summary of each item, filtered to one neighbourhood and linked to the clerk's original. The city publishes the packet but no plain layer or street filter; a news site editorialises; a group chat is unchecked.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: existing nouns, "a plain-language digest of the city council agenda" with "alerts"; nothing to teach beyond "digest" and "alert", both in the subhead.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: residents who care what happens on their street but will not open a 200-page PDF; also clerks who want the packet to stay the record. Not for people who want an opinion or a campaign (summaries never take sides), not for anyone needing the legal text (they get the link to the original), and not yet for Spanish-only readers (English only so far, said beside the form).
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet; the brief supplies no interviews, tickets or reviews, so the hero uses the brief's own words ("plain language", "their street or topic comes up", "open the original agenda item").
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: "will this spam me, cost me, or sell my address?" answered beside the button: "Always free. One email per meeting, only when something matches. Unsubscribe in one click." The Spanish-speaking third gets the language status in the same line, in both languages.
- Conversion action and destination: "Get alerts for my neighbourhood" (neighbourhood select + email). No backend exists, so the submit validates locally and shows a status that says plainly nothing was sent or stored and what the button will do once the list is live. The client must supply the mailing-list endpoint (see Remaining weaknesses).
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Is it accurate and neutral if a model wrote it?" → section 2: item 6.B shown as the clerk's wording beside the model's draft with the volunteer's struck and added text, the volunteer's five checks, and the correction rule.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "Does it cover my street, and how often will it email me?" → the hero digest: choosing a neighbourhood in the form highlights the items that would send an alert (including the zero-match state for Westgate), plus the sample alert email and the matching, timing and frequency facts beside it.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "Can I read the real thing?" → every item carries "Open item N in the packet", which reaches `sample-agenda-item.html`, a labelled stand-in for the city's own page; the record-stays-the-record block addresses clerks directly.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: functional entrance. The product is input (a neighbourhood) to output (plain summaries and an alert), the sample is safe to run locally, and the recipe's "input → output → how it works → limits → final action" order matches what residents, clerks and funders each need to see.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: display and body in a Helvetica-class system stack (`"TeX Gyre Heros","Helvetica Neue",Helvetica,Arial`), the face of public notices and municipal signage, neutral by habit; a Times-class serif (`"TeX Gyre Termes","Times New Roman"`) reserved for the council's own wording, which is what agenda packets are typed in, so the serif-to-sans change is the product's promise made visible; a system mono for item numbers, tags and stamps, because they are identifiers. No web fonts (the page must open from disk offline), weights 400 and 700 only.
- Composition and grid: 1180px max width; hero as two equal columns (copy and form left, the digest right) collapsing to one column at 1000px with the form before the digest; section two as two equal sheets (packet, marked draft) then two text columns; section three as 11:9 (sample email, facts); final section 1:1 (claim, form). Square corners and 1px rules throughout, a document rather than a card deck.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: warm light grey (#f3f2ee) ground with white panels for anything that is a document (digest, packet, draft, email), because the product lives between paper packets and email; near-black ink; one highlighter yellow (#ffe45c) that marks only a match or an added line, and the ink-filled button for the action. No party colours, no illustration.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the sample digest, four agenda items in the published format. Choosing a neighbourhood in the form highlights the first sentence of each matching item, shows an "Alert: <neighbourhood>" chip and updates a status line (including a zero-match state for Westgate). Submitting the form validates locally and prints an honest result. All content is in the HTML before any script runs.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero with digest → how a summary is made and checked (with the packet-stays-the-record block for clerks) → what an alert is and how matching works → final sign-up → footer. Omitted on purpose: a team or "about" section (no names were supplied and none may be invented) and a partners or testimonials strip (none exist).
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: the select-to-highlight is the only interaction that matters; motion is limited to a 180ms background-colour transition on the highlight so the match reads as a pen stroke, plus smooth anchor scrolling, both only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`.

## Two hero variants before code
- Axis the variants differ on (structure, density, emphasis, type or voice): structure.
- Category default and anti-default this plan avoids: default for a civic-tech nonprofit is a blue-and-white page with a city-hall or skyline illustration, three icon cards (Summarise / Alert / Verify), a "Sign up" button and a "trusted by cities" strip. The anti-default is a newspaper pastiche: black masthead, oversized serif headline, paper texture, columns. The plan uses neither: a public-notice sans with the serif confined to quoted council wording, no illustration, the digest itself as the only visual, and one highlighter colour tied to a real state.
