# DESIGN-RECORD

Page: `index.html` (opens from disk; `styles.css`, `script.js`, `icon.svg`, `icon-180.png`, `og.png` beside it). Built in Build mode from `BRIEF.md` with the startup-landing-design skill v0.4.

## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: two sides of one marketplace, licensed commercial kitchens (supply) and small food makers (demand, the side the founder hears from most); category "a booking site for licensed kitchen hours"; business model unknown (rent per shift, fee undecided); no brand files, only the name Ovenhour and the real URL ovenhour.example/kitchens.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): a small food maker who needs a certified space gets one shift in a licensed kitchen, with the equipment and house rules known before booking, because kitchens list the hours they sit dark on one shared sheet.
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: for makers, a monthly commissary membership, cold-calling restaurants, or cooking at home without a permit (doing nothing); for kitchens, leaving the room dark after service.
- The one advantage only this product has: idle hours of an already-licensed kitchen are sold by the shift, with the equipment list and house rules carried on the booking itself; a commissary sells months, a restaurant deal is made by phone.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: "booking site" (existing frame) for "licensed kitchen hours" (a subcategory); the first screen pays for it with one subhead sentence and the example sheet.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: makers who already sell (bakers, caterers, meal-prep, sauces) and need a permitted space a few nights a week; kitchens with a health permit and predictable dark hours. Not for people without a food business, kitchens that run 24 hours, or anyone who needs a kitchen in a county with no listings yet.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet; the brief supplies no interviews or tickets. The hero adapts the founder's own phrasing ("empty hours", "exactly what equipment they get") instead.
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: kitchens fear strangers in their room before they are ready, so the line beside the button says sign-up is open now and no maker can book yet; makers fear a dead end, so the line beside their action says no account and that the note goes to the founders' inbox with their county on it.
- Conversion action and destination: primary "Publish your kitchen's hours" → https://ovenhour.example/kitchens (the only real destination the brief gives). Secondary "Tell us the shift you need" → the maker form (#makers), which composes a mailto to makers@ovenhour.example and shows the note locally.
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "What do I actually get on the night?" → the example shift sheet: a week roster of listed hours, the equipment list, what is off limits, house rules and the permit line, readable as the kitchen or as a maker.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "Will my county's food-safety rules even allow this?" → the county section: the permit and county sit on the sheet, launch counties are not chosen, and the maker form asks for the county first.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "What does it cost, and is there anything to browse yet?" → the pricing section (illustrative $38/h on the sheet labelled as such; Ovenhour's fee not set) and the "Example listing" stamp plus "No kitchen is listed yet" line on the sheet itself.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: workflow theater. The value is a multi-step operational decision (list → book → arrive) with a visible artifact that both sides read; the recipe's "one fictional work item moves through selectable states" became one shift sheet read as the kitchen (list/hold hours) and as a maker (pick a shift).
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: display and text in one grotesk stack ("Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, TeX Gyre Heros, Arial), weights 400/500/600/700, because kitchen signage and tickets are plain sans; data (hours, day names, field labels, the stamp) in the system mono stack, because a kitchen roster and a ticket printer are monospaced. No web fonts: the page must open from disk with no network, and Inter/Poppins (the installed alternatives) are the everywhere faces TY-10 warns about.
- Composition and grid: 1240px wrap; hero top as two columns at ≥1100px (headline left, subhead and two action rows right) so the sheet enters the first screen; below it the ledger, 220px | fluid sheet | 220px; the sheet itself is a document: head, roster (rows = days, x = 06:00→06:00), panel (summary line plus four labelled lists). Sections alternate ground and paper with hairline rules; the steps are three ruled columns.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: a cool pale grey (#eceeeb, stainless and tile, not cream) with near-black ink and white paper for the sheet; one amber (#e39a2e) means "listed hours" wherever it appears (blocks, legend, the step fragments) and a darker amber (#9c6210, 5:1 on white) carries the headline's second line and the example stamp. Service hours are mid grey; held hours are white with a dashed rule; a selected shift is ink with amber text.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the shift sheet. A role switch ("Read as the kitchen / a maker") changes the panel's field labels and the roster's click behaviour: as the kitchen, clicking a block holds it back (dashed, "held") and the summary line recounts blocks and hours; as a maker, clicking a listed block makes it "Your shift" with hours and the illustrative price, and held blocks are not bookable. Every state is plain HTML before any script runs; buttons are real buttons with aria-pressed and descriptive labels; the panel summary is a role=status live region.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero with the ledger (#sheet) → "A shift carries its hours, its equipment and its house rules" (three steps with close-up fragments) → "Food-safety rules differ by county…" with the maker form (#county, #makers) → "Kitchens set the shift price. Ovenhour's fee is not set yet" (#pricing) → final action pair → footer. Omitted on purpose: a "For kitchens / For makers" split with two feature lists, which would have been the two-websites-stapled-together page the brief forbids; both sides read the same sheet instead.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: clicking the role switch and the roster blocks. Motion is colour only: 160ms background/colour/border transitions on blocks and buttons inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; nothing moves, nothing runs by itself, so there is no pause control to need.

## Structural revision
- Weakest explanation or most template-like decision: in the first render the headline block ran the full width (three lines at 76px over a 14ch measure), so at 1440×900 the first screen ended at the sheet's head and the roster, the actual product proof, began at about 980px; the sheet was below the fold on the page's main viewport, and the ledger's side columns sat beside the sheet's head rather than its content. - Change made (structure, not styling): the hero top became two columns at ≥1100px, headline left and subhead plus two button-and-reason rows right, which lifts the ledger by about 200px so the role switch and the first three roster rows render inside 900px; on screens under 720px the sheet now comes first in the ledger and both side notes follow it.
