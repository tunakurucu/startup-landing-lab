One listing, written by the kitchen, read by the maker

There aren't two products here. The kitchen fills in a page once; the maker reads the same page before booking. If something isn't on it, it isn't part of the shift. That one rule is what keeps both sides honest with each other.

Sample listing. There are no kitchens on Ovenhour yet. This is the shape a real one will take.

If you run the kitchen

  • List the hours you're closed, not the hours you'd like to be paid for. A maker will turn up at the start of them.
  • Listed equipment is everything a maker may touch. Leave off anything you don't want used.
  • House rules are enforced as written, so write them the way you'd say them to a new hire.

If you're the maker

  • Hours are exact. The shift ends when the listing says it does, clean-down included.
  • If a piece of equipment isn't listed, it isn't yours for the night.
  • Booking means agreeing to the house rules, so read them before you do.
  • The county decides what permits you need to cook here. Ovenhour doesn't.

Where you cook sets the rules

Food safety is regulated county by county. What a maker needs in order to cook in a shared commercial kitchen, and what they can sell afterwards, is decided by the local health department, and it changes across a county line.

Ovenhour hasn't announced launch counties yet. When a county opens, its requirements for makers will be written out next to the kitchens listed there, before anyone can book. Until then we won't guess on your behalf, and neither should a listing.

The kitchen's license covers the building and the equipment. Whatever a county asks of the person cooking is the maker's to hold, and a kitchen can ask to see it.

Things that commonly differ from one county to the next

  • Whether a cottage-food registration is enough, or a full commercial food permit is needed
  • Who on the shift needs a food-handler card or a certified food manager
  • How a maker's use of a shared kitchen is recorded with the health department
  • Labelling and allergen rules for what you sell
  • Where the finished food can be sold: markets, online, wholesale
  • Whether the kitchen or the maker carries which insurance
