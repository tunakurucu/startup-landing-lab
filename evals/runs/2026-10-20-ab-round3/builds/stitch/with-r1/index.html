    The same picture at 12, 22 and 40 stitches across

    You choose the stitch count before charting. Fewer stitches means a quicker, blockier piece; more means detail and a longer evening. The finished size is worked out from your fabric count.

    All three are charted from the same drawing by the same code that runs the sample editor. Sizes are stitch count divided by fabric count.

    From a photo to a chart in three steps

    Nothing here is hidden. Each step is a decision you can see and change.

    1. The photo is cut into stitches

      You pick the stitch count. Each square of the grid takes the average colour of the picture under it, so fine lines get blurred. That is normal and it is why step three exists.

    2. Each square is matched to a thread you can buy

        The colours are grouped into the number of threads you asked for. Each group is then matched to the nearest shade in a thread catalogue, so the thread list is something you can take to a shop.

      • You fix what the averaging lost

        A stem, an eye, a whisker: averages lose small things. Pick a thread and put them back square by square. That is the editor at the top of this page, at full size in the beta.

      For the photo on your fridge, and for the patterns you sell

      Stitching for yourself

      Start from your own picture: a dog, a front door, a holiday. The chart is yours to simplify. Drop threads you don't own, swap a shade for one in your box, and unpick the squares you'd rather leave bare.

      The sample editor on this page uses a drawing. The beta editor charts your photos.

      Making patterns to sell

      Chart view is the symbol chart and thread list a buyer stitches from, and every square in it was placed on purpose. Thread counts update as you edit, so the list is right when you ship.
