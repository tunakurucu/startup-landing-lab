How a shift changes hands

  1. 11:48 pm

    The closing manager writes it down

    Before they leave. Short lines, in their own words, on their phone, standing at the pass. It takes about as long as a text.

  2. 9:02 am

    The opening manager reads it on the walk in

    Before the first delivery and the first call-in, they already know what’s 86’d, who’s short, and what the service tech still hasn’t called back about.

  3. Sunday night

    The owner reads the whole week

    On a laptop, in the office or at home. Every shift, in order, in one place. Nothing to chase down, nobody to call.

What goes in the log

Every entry gets one of four marks, so the opener can skim for what matters to them first. The marks on the page are the same ones you saw in the handover above.

  • Ran out

    What you 86’d, what’s running low, what to add to the next order.

  • Called in

    Who’s out, who’s covering, which shifts are still open.

  • Needs fixing

    What’s broken, who you called about it, and the workaround until they show up.

  • Heads up

    The inspector’s card. The regular who complained. The thing you would otherwise put in the group chat and lose by Friday.

Why not the group chat?

The group chat scrolls away. The sticky note falls off the screen. The binder stays at the host stand, where the closer isn’t and the owner never is.

Shiftnote is the binder, on everyone’s phone, that doesn’t get lost. One log per restaurant. Everyone who runs a shift can write in it and read it. That’s the whole product.
