    How it works

    From photo to floss in three steps

    1. 1 Drop in a photo

      Pick how wide the piece should be in stitches and how many threads you're willing to buy. Crossling sizes the photo to the grid.

    2. 2 Every square gets a real thread

      Colours are matched to a catalogue of threads you can actually buy, by number, not to whatever hex value the pixel happened to be.

    3. 3 Fix it before you stitch it

      Auto-conversion gets you most of the way. The last stretch is yours: smooth a jagged edge, fill a region, merge two near-identical threads, then download the chart.

    Real thread colours

    A chart is only useful if the shop sells the colour

    Most photo converters quantise to arbitrary RGB values and leave you to guess which skein is closest. Crossling only ever picks from named catalogue threads, so the key you print is the shopping list you take to the store, and the colour you stitch is the colour you previewed.

    When the matcher picks two threads that are nearly identical, that costs you a skein for a handful of stitches. Select one in the editor and swap it for the other; the whole pattern updates.

    Questions

    The ones we keep getting

    Is the beta free?

    Yes. While it's a beta, Crossling is free to use, and we'll say in advance if that changes. Expect rough edges and the occasional moved button.

    Which threads does it use?

    Named, numbered threads from the common catalogues stitchers already buy. The editor on this page uses a short sample of the range so the demo stays quick; the beta editor matches against the full catalogue.

    What happens to my photo?

    On this page, nothing leaves your browser; the conversion runs locally. The beta editor keeps your photo only as long as it takes to build the pattern, and the pattern itself is yours to download and delete.

    Can I sell patterns I make with it?

    We haven't decided the commercial terms yet, and we'd rather not pretend otherwise. The section above explains what's settled and what isn't. If this matters to you, email us; it's the input we need most.

    Why not just use a free pixel converter?

    You can, and for a rough first pass they're fine. The difference is what happens after: a converter gives you pixels, Crossling gives you a chart with thread numbers you can edit square by square, so the hours you spend stitching go into a pattern you've already checked.

    Does it handle dark or low-contrast photos?

    Not magically. Limiting the thread count forces the matcher to choose, which often helps; so does cropping to the part you actually want to stitch. When it still looks muddy, that's what the fill tool and thread swap are for.
