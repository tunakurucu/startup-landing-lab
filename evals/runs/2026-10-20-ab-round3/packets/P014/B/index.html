Set it once before bed. The light does the rest, slowly.

There is nothing to pair and nothing to tap. The dial on the base is the whole interface: it sets the time you want to be up, and Morrow works back thirty minutes from there.

  1. Turn the dial to the time you want to be up.Ten o’clock on a Sunday or six on a Monday; it is one turn either way. That sentence is the manual.
  2. Sleep. The lamp stays off.No clock face, no standby light, nothing glowing at three in the morning. Nothing to pair or update, either.
  3. Thirty minutes before your time, the light begins.A faint ember first, then orange, amber, gold.
  4. At your time, the room is full morning.No app was involved at any point.

A sound alarm takes one second. Morrow takes thirty minutes, on purpose.

An alarm goes from silence to noise in no time at all, and so do you. Morrow goes from dark to morning over half an hour, which is long enough that nothing about it is sudden. The first minutes are too dim to notice. The last minutes are hard to sleep through.

That is the whole idea. We built it because we wanted to wake up this way ourselves, with nothing on the nightstand that needs charging, updating, or looking at.

You will find no sleep-science claims on this page. We haven’t run a study, so we won’t quote one. Morrow is a lamp that gets brighter before the time you set; it is not a medical or sleep device.
