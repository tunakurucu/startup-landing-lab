What the volunteer checks before signing

  1. Every sentence traces to a line in the packet.
  2. No opinion words: good, bad, finally, unfortunately, controversial.
  3. The kind of decision is named: first reading, final vote, hearing, consent item.
  4. Streets, dates, dollar figures and item numbers match the packet.
  5. The link opens the item in the city's own packet.

A summary never says whether a decision is good or bad, never guesses what the council will do, and never adds anything a resident could not find in the packet. If a published summary turns out to be wrong, a correction goes to the same list under the same item number.

The city's packet stays the record; every summary links to it

A summary is a way in, not a replacement. Each one links to the item in the agenda packet the city publishes, so you can read the full text, the staff report and the attachments yourself. Commonread does not host copies of city documents and does not speak for the city or the council.

For city clerks: summaries are drafted from the packet as posted, and only after it is posted. Nothing in your record is rewritten, and the link goes to your page. If the packet format changes, summaries pause rather than guess.

An alert is one short email, sent only when your neighbourhood is on the agenda

You choose one neighbourhood. When an item names a street or area inside it, you get the summary, the meeting time, and the link to the original, the day the agenda is posted.

How an item is matched to you
By the street names and address ranges in the item, checked against the neighbourhood boundaries the city publishes. An item that names no place is marked citywide and listed in one line at the bottom of every alert.
When it arrives
The day the agenda is posted. The email says how many days are left before the meeting and how to sign up to speak.
How often
At most one email per meeting, and none when nothing in your neighbourhood is on the agenda. No newsletters, no asks.
Your address
Used only to send these alerts. Not sold, not shared, and deleted when you unsubscribe.
Language
Summaries and alerts are in English for now. About a third of the pilot city speaks Spanish at home, and Spanish is the next thing on the list; volunteers who can check a Spanish summary are the step that unlocks it.
