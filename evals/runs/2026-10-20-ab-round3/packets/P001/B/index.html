Kitchens close at ten. Licenses don't.

Ovenhour is a booking site where licensed commercial kitchens rent their empty hours to small food makers. A kitchen lists idle hours, house rules and equipment; a maker books one shift and sees exactly what comes with it.

If you make food

  1. Book one block, not a month.
  2. See the ovens, the mixer and the walk-in space before you book.
  3. The permit, the county and the rules you agree to are on the sheet.

A shift carries its hours, its equipment and its house rules

Both sides read the same sheet. The kitchen writes it once; the maker sees it before booking and again on the night.

  1. 1

    The kitchen marks its dark hours

    Whole blocks, not minutes. A block the kitchen holds back never shows to a maker. Service hours stay the kitchen's own.

    service 09:00–21:3022:00–06:00

  2. 2

    The equipment list is the contract

    Counts and sizes, not brands. Two ovens means two ovens. What is off limits is written next to what is not.

    2 convection ovens60-qt mixerfryer: off limits

  3. 3

    A maker books one block

    The booking carries the rules, the equipment list and the permit line. Nothing has to be agreed by phone.

    Thu 22:00–06:00 · 8 h

Food-safety rules differ by county, so every listing shows its permit

A commercial kitchen is permitted by its county health department, and what you may make there depends on that permit and on your own. So the permit type and the county sit on the sheet, above the price, before you book.

We have not chosen launch counties. Makers are the people we hear from most, and where they cook will decide where kitchens are asked to list first.
