A water heater that waits for cheap power.

Tankshift is a controller an electrician adds to your electric water heater. It holds heating back until your tariff's cheap hours and fills the tank before you need it, so showers stay hot and peak hours stay quiet.

    Three rules, and the one that overrides the other two

    It runs the same day after day. The tariff hours are set once at install, from the utility's published schedule. The homeowner does not need an app.

    1. Fill the tank before each peak block

      In the two hours before peak price starts, it heats the tank to its full setpoint, whatever the thermostat would have done. The tank goes into the peak full.

    2. Hold through the peak

      While the peak price runs, the element stays off. Showers, dishes and laundry draw on what was stored. In the cheap hours it heats as the thermostat asks.

    3. Top up if the tank runs low, even at peak price

      If a heavy evening pulls the tank below its reserve, it heats anyway, at the peak price, and logs that it did. A hot shower outranks a cheap one. You can see those top-ups in the example above.

    What it never does. It does not change the thermostat setting or the water temperature, and it does not add a sensor inside the tank. It switches the heater's own circuit, so the heater's thermostat and high-limit cut-out stay in charge of safety.

    The tank-level line in the example is the model's estimate of stored heat. The real controller works from the heater's call for heat and the time of day; it does not measure water temperature.

    For electricians: one visit, on the circuit the heater already has

    Tankshift goes inline between the heater's disconnect and the tank. No new circuit, no plumbing, and the heater keeps its own thermostat.

    1. 01

      Check the heaterA resistive electric tank heater with its own thermostat. Not gas, not tankless, not a heat pump unit with a built-in schedule.

    2. 02

      Power off at the disconnect, mount inlineThe controller mounts beside the heater and takes the feed after the disconnect. One box, one conduit run, no connection inside the tank.

    3. 03

      Set the tariff hours from the installer sheetThe sheet lists the utility's published peak, shoulder and off-peak hours. Set them once on the unit; there is no app to pair.

    4. 04

      Test the hold and the top-upForce a peak block and confirm the element stays off; force the reserve and confirm it heats. Leave the sheet with the homeowner.

    Enclosure rating, conductor sizes and the installer sheet are published with the safety listing. Until then, nothing on this page replaces the sheet.

    For rebate programmes: a shifted load you can see, without a call to the homeowner

    Each controller keeps a log of when it heated, in which tariff block, and every peak-price top-up with its reason. The homeowner opts in at install. One pilot with a utility is running now; its name stays under the pilot agreement until they publish.

    Price is set with each region's rebate programme

    Hardware and install
    One price per region, published before the first installs there. Where a utility rebate applies, the page for that region will show the price after the rebate.
    Ongoing
    No subscription is planned. The controller works from the tariff hours set at install.
    Changing tariff
    When the utility changes its hours, the installer sheet is updated and the hours can be reset on the unit.
