The batch record is finished at knockout and opens on the owner’s laptop.

Today the sheet is written wet at the kettle, typed up dry in the evening and read a week late. Grainline keeps one record per batch. Each reading the brewer logs lands on it with the time, the target it was checked against and any note, so there is nothing to type up and nothing to decode.

Grainline keeps the brew record. It does not file excise returns; it keeps the record readable for whoever does.

Sample batch record. On a live install the step names, units and target ranges are the brewery’s own.

An off reading gets flagged, not blocked.

Targets come from the recipe, set once per beer. A reading outside its range is marked on the record and the brew day carries on. The brewer adds a note when there is a quiet minute, and the owner sees the flag and the note together, in the row where it happened.

Nothing waits on the app. If a reading is wrong, the brewer changes it from the same step on the tablet.

Early access is for breweries still on paper sheets.

Good fit
A small brewhouse where readings still go on a clipboard or into a spreadsheet after the brew day.
Not for
Breweries with a production system already logging the brewhouse, or anyone who needs excise reporting. Grainline does not do that.
What you get
Grainline on your brewhouse tablet with your own step list and targets, and a direct line to the people building it.
