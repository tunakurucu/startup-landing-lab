One record, two readers.

Today a brew day gets written down twice: in a damp notebook on the floor, then again in a spreadsheet in the office. Grainline keeps one record, made where the readings happen and read wherever they are needed.

On the floor

  • Targets you can hit with a thumb. One reading per screen, big numbers, nothing that needs a dry fingertip or a stylus.
  • Stamped when you take it. Mash temperature at 06:35 is logged at 06:35, not pieced back together at four o’clock.
  • Last time’s numbers beside this time’s. Where the previous batch of this recipe sat at pre-boil is one glance away, not a cupboard away.

In the office

  • Every batch in the same shape. Gravities, volumes, temperatures and times laid out the same way for batch 12 and batch 412.
  • What the brewhouse actually did. Efficiency, boil-off and volume into the fermenter come from the readings, not from the recipe sheet.
  • Written whether or not anyone wrote it up. The record exists the moment the readings do, so nothing is waiting on a quiet afternoon.

What Grainline is not: excise software. It records the brew day. Duty returns, and the paperwork around them, stay with whatever you use for them today.
