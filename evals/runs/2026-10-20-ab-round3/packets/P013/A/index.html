Written for the person who has to approve it

Sales wants the questionnaire back by Friday. Security has to stand behind every line of it. Paperwren is designed around the second job, because that's the one that decides whether the answers go out at all.

Nothing without a source

Each sentence carries a citation to a named section of a named policy version. The passage sits beside the draft, so checking a claim is a glance, not a search through the shared drive.

Gaps are flagged, not filled

When the policies don't cover what was asked, the draft says so and stops. It won't guess a key rotation period, assume a certification, or borrow an answer from a previous questionnaire you didn't write.

You approve, every time

No answer is exported until a reviewer signs it. Your edits are kept with the question, so the next questionnaire starts from what you actually said, not from what the agent first drafted.

For the sales engineer waiting on all this: a draft that arrives already checked gets approved in one pass instead of three. The speed comes from fewer rounds, not from skipping the review.

What a pilot looks like

  1. You bring the last questionnaire you answered and the policies you answered it from.
  2. Paperwren drafts it again. Your security lead reviews the drafts next to the answers you actually sent.
  3. You see where it cited correctly, where it flagged, and where it would have been wrong. You keep the comparison whether or not you continue.
