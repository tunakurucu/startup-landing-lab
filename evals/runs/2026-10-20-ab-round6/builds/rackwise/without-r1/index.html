One trip to the freezer

The search happens at the bench, so the door is only open for the part that has to happen at the freezer.

  1. Look it up before you get up

    Search by sample, box name or the person who stored it. You get the freezer, shelf, rack, box and position.

  2. Open the door knowing where to reach

    Go straight to shelf 3, pull rack C, take out box 4. No lifting lids on every box in the rack to read the labels.

  3. Take the tube from D5

    The position is in the box’s own grid, rows A to I and columns 1 to 9, the way your boxes are already laid out.

  4. Record it with your gloves still on

    A phone on the freezer shelf or in your pocket is enough. Big buttons, one tap: taken out, put back, or used up.

Who it’s for

Lab managers set it up and keep it honest. Everyone else uses it at the freezer, a few times a day.

Lab managers

  • Lay out each freezer the way it really is: shelves, racks, boxes and their grid.
  • See who stored what, and what’s still in the freezer after someone leaves the lab.
  • Plan a defrost or a freezer move and keep every box’s location right while it happens.

Researchers and technicians

  • Find a sample by name, box or person before walking over.
  • Record a tube going in or coming out with one tap, gloves on.
  • Find an empty position for new samples without opening box after box.

Rackwise doesn’t monitor freezer temperature

It has no sensors and sends no temperature alarms. Keep the alarm and monitoring system you already rely on. Rackwise tells you where your samples are, not how cold they are.
