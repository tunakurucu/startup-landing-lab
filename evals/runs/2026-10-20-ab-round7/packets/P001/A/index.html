What it reads at your beach

The same four things you already check, read for the beach you're actually going to, which way it faces and what the water does there.

Tide
Height and whether it's coming in or going out, so you know when there's enough water to swim and when you'll be wading back over the stones.
Wind
Speed and direction against the way your beach faces. Offshore can mean flat water near the shore; onshore is usually what makes it lumpy.
Swell
Height and period. A small, long-period swell and a choppy short one look the same on a map and feel nothing alike in the water.
Water temperature
In degrees Celsius, near your beach. The number everyone asks for when you get out.

When a window closes early, you hear about it

Conditions change between the night before and the morning. If the window you were planning around shrinks or closes, Tidewise tells you, with the reason, so you can go earlier, go elsewhere or leave it for tomorrow.

It won't nudge you to go in. It tells you what changed.

Conditions, not permission

Tidewise is not a lifeguard. It will never tell you it's safe to swim.

A swim window is the stretch of time when the tide, wind and swell readings at your beach line up. It says nothing about currents it can't see, how you feel that morning or what the water looks like when you get there.

You look at the sea, and you decide. Tidewise is there so you arrive knowing what the numbers say.
