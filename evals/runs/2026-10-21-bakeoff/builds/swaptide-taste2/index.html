The whiteboard by the break room

Today a swap starts as a line on the whiteboard, or a message to the unit group text at 02:47, and ends when somebody says yes.

Nobody checks whether Saturday night still has a charge nurse, or whether the nurse picking it up would be on her fourth night in a row.

Often the manager hears about it at handover, after it has already happened, and has to choose between undoing it and signing it off.

Swaptide moves the same conversation onto the phones nurses already carry, and puts the unit’s rules and the manager back into it before anyone changes their plans.

Questions

Does Swaptide handle payroll? No. It records the swap and the manager’s decision. Hours, pay and premiums stay with your payroll and timekeeping systems.

Can it override our union agreement? No. The rules it checks are taken from your unit’s policy and agreements. It can stop a swap that breaks one, or pass it on to the manager. It never approves anything by itself.

Who makes the final decision? The unit manager, every time. A swap that passes every check still waits for the manager, who can decline it.

What happens when a swap breaks a rule? It stops before the manager sees it, and the nurse is told which rule and why, in plain words, so she can try someone else.

Who sets the rules? The unit does. On the pilot call we go through the limits you already use: hours, shifts in a row, rest, charge cover and competencies.

Who approves when the manager is away? Whoever your unit names. We set that up with you at the start of a pilot.

Does it work on nights? It is built around twelve-hour days and nights, including the half-hour handover overlap and the night that ends on the next morning.

How long is a pilot? The first one has run for three months on one medical-surgical unit. One unit for about that long is a sensible start.

Who is it for? Nurse managers and directors of nursing buy it from a department budget. Nurses on the unit use it, usually on a break.

What does the pilot call cover? How swaps happen on your unit now, which rules apply, who approves, and what a pilot would need from you. Pricing too.
