The rules you already have, written down once.

Every dock has rules. Most of them live in the receiving lead's head and on a laminated sheet at the guard shack. Dockline turns the sheet into settings, and carriers can only book what the sheet allows.

Everything on the sheet is a setting.

Change the lunch closure at 16:40 and dispatchers see it at 16:40. Take a door out of service and its windows disappear; the bookings already on it get moved, and their dispatchers get told.

  • Doors are typed: live unload, drop, reefer, flatbed-only. A dry van can't book the reefer door by mistake.
  • Window length is per door. A drop takes 30 minutes; a floor-loaded live unload takes 90. The board knows the difference.
  • Blackouts for lunch, shift change and inventory counts, by door or for the whole building.
  • Limits per carrier, so one broker can't take every morning window on a Monday.
  • Preferred carriers get earlier access to next week's windows. Everyone else sees them Thursday.

Setup takes about an hour with your door list and your hours. We do it with you on the first call.

Dispatchers book in under a minute. No account.

A dispatcher sending trucks to forty consignees will not learn forty portals. So there is nothing to learn: one link, three fields, pick a window.

On the day, the board runs the yard.

Gate

The guard types the reference or scans it off the driver's phone. Dockline shows which door, whether the truck is early, and whether the window is still held.

Early and late

Early trucks wait in a marked lane and get called forward when their door clears. Late past the grace window and the booking releases; the driver is told to rebook, not to park.

Doors that run over

Drag a booking to another door and the dispatcher gets the change. Receiving decides, the carrier finds out, nobody calls anybody.

End of day

Every arrival, check-in and departure time is kept. When a carrier sends a detention invoice, you have the gate stamp.
