# DESIGN-RECORD

Page: `index.html` (opens from disk; no network, no web fonts). Skill: startup-landing-design v0.5, Build mode.

## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: Buyers are operations managers at mid-size warehouses; daily users are carriers' dispatchers on the booking side. Category: dock appointment scheduling (a booking app for dock doors). Business model: sold to the warehouse (demo-led); the carrier side is a free booking link. No brand files supplied; name "Dockline"; two URLs given (demo, booking).
- Promise (who / what changes / how): Warehouse ops managers / trucks arrive at a booked door and time instead of queuing in the yard, and the dock phone and shared spreadsheet go away / the warehouse publishes slots on its doors and carriers' dispatchers book them from a link.
- Five-second line, quoted as shown in the first screen (who + what): "Dockline is a dock appointment app for mid-size warehouses. You publish slots on your doors; carriers' dispatchers book an arrival time instead of calling in and waiting in the yard."
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: phone calls to the receiving office, a shared spreadsheet of expected trucks, and first-come-first-served queuing at the gate (doing nothing). Nearest software alternative: Opendock-style dock scheduling built for large DCs.
- The one advantage only this product has: the booking side is built for the dispatcher, not the warehouse: a link, a door and an hour, no carrier account or training (brief: "must find it easy"); and the whole product is the dock door, not a module of a yard-management suite. Both are design goals the page states as goals, not measured results.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: existing ("dock appointment"), framed as "a dock appointment app for mid-size warehouses"; costs one sentence, carried by the five-second line.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: mid-size warehouses (a handful to a few dozen doors) that still run receiving by phone and spreadsheet. Not for: large DCs that already run a YMS/TMS with integrated dock scheduling, or yards that need gate cameras, yard-jockey dispatch or WMS integration on day one.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet. The brief's own phrases ("phone calls, a shared spreadsheet and trucks waiting for hours outside the gate") are adapted in the five-second line and the hero note.
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: a sales call costs time and commits you. Line beside the button: "30 minutes, online. Bring your door count and receiving hours." (30 minutes is an assumption to confirm; see Interview log.)
- Conversion action and destination: "Book a demo" → https://dockline.example/demo (header, hero, final section). Secondary path for dispatchers: "Carriers: book a slot" → https://book.dockline.example.
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Will my carriers' dispatchers actually use it instead of calling?" → the booking grid in the first screen is operable: pick an open hour under a door, enter carrier and load ref, confirm; the trailer appears at that door on the yard clock. Plus the three-step booking section and the example confirmation the driver gets.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "What does my day look like, and what happens when a truck is late?" → the gate's receiving list for the sample day with statuses (checked in, at door, due, late with the hold time, departed), and the slot-rules sheet with a hold-after-start rule per door.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "What does it cost and what will setup take?" → the practical section: cost per warehouse by door count (quoted on the demo; no public list yet), setup is a door list and receiving hours, and what it leaves alone (WMS, TMS, yard jockey).

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: Expressive world. The product lives in one physical setting and the brief asks for a page that understands it; the world holds the booking grid as a working part (Railway's principle of the metaphor as structure, Cal.com's principle of the customer-facing artifact in the hero).
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: Display: a condensed bold grotesque from the system (Helvetica Neue Condensed Bold / Arial Narrow / TeX Gyre Heros Cn / Roboto Condensed), the voice of stencilled door numbers and dock signage; tracking 0. Text: Helvetica/Arial-class system sans at 17px, 1.5. Data: ui-monospace (SF Mono / Menlo / Consolas / Liberation Mono) with tabular figures for times, references and the grid. No web fonts: the page must open from disk with no network, so system stacks are the honest choice; recorded as a constraint, not a reflex.
- Composition and grid: 1200px content width; hero as a 5/7 split (copy / world) where the booking sheet occupies the copy column's second row; sections below use the content width for prose and the full 1200 for tables; the gate section is a dark band (the wall colour) to change material where the reading task changes from booking to supervising.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: light concrete grey (#e8e9e5), not white and not cream, because the product is used on an apron of concrete; wall and booked cells in dark steel (#2c3036); action green (#157a3a) taken from the dock light's "clear to back in"; dock-light red and green only on the lamps; safety yellow only on the painted dock-edge line. Flat tone steps, no gradients, no shadows; rules and overlap carry depth.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the dock face with a yard-clock slider (06:00–16:00 in quarter hours) and a 6 × 10 booking grid beneath it. Moving the clock places trailers at doors whose booked hour covers that time and lights those doors red. Clicking an open hour opens a sheet (carrier, load ref, trailer type); confirming fills the cell, gives a sample reference, announces it in a status region, and the trailer appears when the clock reaches the hour. Hours earlier than the clock are closed and say why when clicked. A reset restores the sample day. Labelled "Sample day: illustrative; nothing is sent anywhere."
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: 1 hero world and grid → 2 how a dispatcher books and what the driver gets (three steps and an example confirmation) → 3 the slot rules you set once (table) → 4 what the gate sees (receiving list, dark band) → 5 cost, setup and what it leaves alone → 6 final action. Omitted on purpose: a feature grid and any proof/logo section, because there are no customers to name and no results to show.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: booking an hour. Motion only confirms state: a trailer entering its door (360ms transform ease-out, from the yard), a 150ms opacity change on cells and lamps; nothing moves on its own.
