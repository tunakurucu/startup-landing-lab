# DESIGN-RECORD

Page: `index.html` (static, opens from disk). Mode: Build. Skill v0.5, workflow-theater recipe, quiet statement level.

## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: controllers and accounting managers at 200–2,000-employee US companies, with CFOs and auditors reading over their shoulder; category is unclaimed property (escheat) compliance software; sold through an assessment call (B2B, sales-led); no brand assets supplied, no logos or outcomes to show; must not read as legal advice.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): for a controller who found the obligation through an audit letter, every uncashed check and dormant balance gets a state and a date instead of sitting on the bank rec, because Holdfast runs each item on its state's dormancy clock and builds the state's report.
- Five-second line, quoted as shown in the first screen (who + what): "unclaimed property software for finance teams" (the subhead opens "Holdfast is unclaimed property software for finance teams.").
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: doing nothing (stale checks written back to income until an audit letter arrives), a once-a-year spreadsheet off the bank rec plus the state's holder portal, or a consultancy hired after the letter.
- The one advantage only this product has: it starts from the artifacts the controller already has (the outstanding-check list and AR credits) and keeps every item on a per-state clock continuously, so the deadline is known before the audit letter rather than reconstructed after it (inference from the brief; the brief gives tracking + per-state rules + report preparation).
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: existing, "unclaimed property software"; costs nothing to teach a controller, and the subhead spells out the three jobs for a CFO who has not met the term.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: multi-state payers with 200–2,000 employees and no unclaimed-property program; not for companies that need audit representation (a law firm) or a tax department already filing in every state. Both stated on the page beside the call description.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet; the brief supplies only "usually discover the obligation after an audit letter", which the call section adapts.
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: a sales call costs time and commits you; "The call is free. Bring your outstanding-check list and we show you which states it already touches." sits under both buttons (free call is an assumption, see Interview log).
- Conversion action and destination: "Request an assessment call" → https://holdfast.example/assessment (header, hero, closing block, footer; same label everywhere).
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Do we even have an obligation, and how big?" → the hero register: 12 sample checks re-sorted from bank-rec order into Past due / Report by / Clock still running, with totals, and a summary block beside the headline.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "Are the dates right? Rules change and a wrong one is a fine." → the state-rule table (dormancy, period end, report date, last-checked date for 8 sample states) with the not-legal-advice line beside it, and the five-date timeline for one check showing how a date is derived.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "What do I actually get, and what happens on the call?" → the sample California notice report (holder, period, property line, total) and the three-step description of the assessment call with Built for / Not for / Cost.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: workflow theater. The value unfolds through states (issued → stale → dormant → letter → reported) and one work item (check 10492) recurs across the hero, the timeline, the report and the rule table; the buyer makes an operational decision with visible artifacts.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: display Charter (Matthew Carter's 1987 face drawn for laser-printed documents, the typeface register of printed financial statements; system stack Charter / Bitstream Charter / Georgia so no font file is downloaded) for the claim and section headings; text Inter / Helvetica / system sans for explanation and UI; data ui-monospace stack with tabular figures for money, check numbers and dates so the ledger reads as a ledger. Two registers (document serif, ledger mono) anchored by the same hairline rules and ink colour.
- Composition and grid: 1320px max width, left-aligned, H1 across the top, then a 4/8 split (explanation narrow, artifact wide); sections alternate a 7/5 heading-and-lead row with a wide artifact (timeline, 8/4 table-and-aside, 880px report, 7/5 steps-and-fit); density runs open → dense → quiet → active → short.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: paper #f6f6f3 (a cool near-white, the controller's spreadsheet and bank statement, not cream) with white sheets for artifacts and hairline rules for structure; no shadows. One action colour, deep green #0b5a3c, on the primary action and on "due" state; red #9b1c1c only for "past due"; everything else ink and grey.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the register. Twelve dated sample checks with payee, amount and last-known-address state. A segmented control sorts them as the bank rec lists them or as Holdfast files them; the sorted view groups into Past due / Report to {state} by {date} · {days} / Clock still running with per-group totals. An as-of date (bounded 2020–2035, refuses with a reason) and a state-of-incorporation select (moves the two unaddressed checks) recompute everything, a reset restores the sample, and a status region announces the result. Rules are an eight-state sample labelled beside the table. Check 10492 is marked "followed below" and recurs in the timeline.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero (register) → one check, five dates (timeline) → the rule behind each date (table + no-address and clock notes) → the report in the state's format (sample) → what the assessment call covers (steps, built for / not for / cost) → closing action → footer. Omitted on purpose: a features/integrations grid, and any testimonial or logo row (none exist).
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: selecting the sort, changing the as-of date or the incorporation state; the only motion is a 180ms opacity dip on the table container to mark the swap (none under reduced motion), plus 160ms colour transitions on buttons. Nothing moves on its own.
