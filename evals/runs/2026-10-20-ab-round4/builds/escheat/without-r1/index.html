If the audit letter has already arrived

Most companies learn about unclaimed property the same way: a state, or an audit firm working for several states on a contingent fee, writes to ask for records. The lookback can run ten years or more, and the first request usually covers every uncashed check, credit balance and refund in that period.

What the state is looking for
Checks that were never cashed, customer credits that were never used or refunded, and rebates or payroll that never reached the person. Each becomes the state's to hold once it has sat unclaimed for that state's dormancy period.
What you need in hand
Bank statements and check registers for the lookback period, accounts payable and payroll histories, customer account balances, and any prior filings. Gaps in the records are usually filled by estimate, which rarely favors the holder.
Where Holdfast fits
We take the records, age every item against the rules of the state entitled to it, and produce the schedules the examiner will ask for. Counsel handles the audit itself. We are glad to join those calls with the numbers.

How it works

Three stages, in the order the states expect them. Each one leaves a record of what was done and when.

  1. 1

    Bring in the records

    Import outstanding-check lists from your bank, the AP and payroll check registers, customer credit balances from AR, and open refunds or rebates. CSV exports are enough; nothing has to change in your ERP.

    Holdfast matches each item to the owner's last known address, which decides which state can claim it.

  2. 2

    Age every item by state

    Each item is placed on the clock of its state and property type. A vendor check in one state may be dormant after three years; a payroll check in another after one. Items that cross the line are listed as reportable, with the date they crossed it.

    Reconciliation is built in: voided, reissued and cashed items drop off with a note of why.

  3. 3

    Notify owners, then file

    Owner notices go out inside each state's window, and responses are logged against the items. What remains is compiled into the state's electronic format with the cover forms, ready to submit with the remittance.

    Every filing is kept with its source items, so the next audit starts with a complete record instead of a reconstruction.

State rules, with their sources

Dormancy periods, notice windows and due dates differ by state and by property type, and legislatures change them. A wrong date is a penalty, so Holdfast does not show a date without saying where it came from and when it was last checked.

  • Each state entry cites the statute and administrative guidance it was read from.
  • Each entry carries the date it was last verified and the name of the reviewer.
  • When a state changes a rule, every affected item in your calendar is updated and the change is written to the entry's log, with the old value kept.
  • Nothing is published to customers until a second reviewer has signed off.

Holdfast is software, not a law firm. The state entries are our reading of each statute, maintained for use in the product. They are not legal advice. Decisions about your company's obligations, including whether to approach a state voluntarily, belong with your counsel.

Questions controllers ask

The ones that come up in nearly every first call.

We have never filed. Is it too late?

No, but the order of operations matters. Several states offer voluntary disclosure programs that limit lookback and waive penalties if you approach them before they approach you. Whether to use one is a legal decision; Holdfast gives you and your counsel the numbers to make it.

Which state gets a given check?

The state of the owner's last known address on your books. If there is no address, it goes to your state of incorporation. Holdfast applies these priority rules to every item and shows which one was used.

How do you keep deadlines current?

Each state entry is read from the statute and the state's holder guidance, carries a verification date and reviewer, and is reviewed again on a schedule tied to each state's legislative session. Changes update every affected date on your calendar and are logged with the previous value. See state rules, with their sources.

Does Holdfast file for us?

Holdfast prepares the electronic files, cover forms and remittance summaries. Submission through each state's portal is done by your team or by ours on your behalf, as you prefer. Either way the submission receipt is stored with the filing.

Is any of this legal advice?

No. Holdfast is software built by people who have prepared these reports. It applies published rules to your records and shows its work. Questions about your obligations, audit strategy or voluntary disclosure go to your counsel, and we are glad to join those conversations.

An annual subscription priced on the number of states you report to and the volume of items. We quote it in writing at the end of the assessment call, with nothing added later.
