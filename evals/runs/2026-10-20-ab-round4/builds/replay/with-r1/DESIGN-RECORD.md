## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: Backend engineers on Node.js services who lose afternoons to flaky tests; category: a test record-and-replay CLI (open source, MIT, npm install); business model: free today, a hosted team version is planned and not built; no brand files supplied, so the wordmark, mark and palette are new and provisional.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): For backend engineers with tests that fail in CI and pass locally, the CI failure now fails the same way on their laptop, because Rerun records the run's clock, network responses, random draws and environment in CI and feeds them back on replay.
- Five-second line, quoted as shown in the first screen (who + what): "Rerun is a free command-line tool for backend engineers. It records a failing test run in CI and replays it on your machine: same inputs, same timing, same network responses."
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: retry the job until it goes green (`--retries`, re-run button), add sleeps, quarantine the test, or reproduce by hand from the CI log; doing nothing (live with the flake) is common. Nearest tools: flaky-test detectors (BuildPulse, Trunk Flaky Tests) that report flakiness but do not replay; Replay.io / rr, which record a browser or native process rather than a CI test run.
- The one advantage only this product has: the CI run itself comes to the laptop with its inputs pinned (clock, network, randomness, env), so the failure reproduces instead of being guessed at.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: subcategory "test record-and-replay CLI" built on the known phrase "record and replay"; it costs one sentence (the five-second line) and the "What gets recorded" section.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: best fit: engineers on Node.js backends with CI pipelines that already upload artifacts. Not for: Go/Java/Python services, browser/UI test flakes, or races between processes (stated on the page under Limits).
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet; the brief supplies no interviews, tickets or reviews. The hero adapts the brief's own phrasing "flaky tests that pass locally" into "It fails in CI. Now it fails on your laptop too." The record lists collecting three real quotes as a client task.
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: "What does this cost and what do I have to set up?" → beside the primary action: "Free and MIT-licensed. No account needed." plus the visible one-line install command.
- Conversion action and destination: "Copy install command" copies `npm install -g @rerun/cli` (confirmed in a live region); the lighter path is "Read the docs" → https://rerun.example/docs and GitHub → https://github.example/rerun/rerun.
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Does it really reproduce the failure, or just rerun the test?" → the twin-track tape in the hero: a recorded CI run (7 events) replayed on a laptop track, ending in the identical assertion failure, with an "npm test (no rerun)" mode showing the same test passing locally for comparison.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "How can it pin my clock and network, and will it work with my stack?" → the "The test is not random. Its inputs are." section (four interception points with what is recorded and replayed) and the Limits section (Node.js only, no cross-process races, no child processes).
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "What do I have to change in CI, and what does it cost?" → the two-command terminal example (`npx rerun record -- npm test`, `rerun replay ci-8841`) labelled as an example, and the $0 pricing card with the planned hosted version stated as not built.

- Archetype and why: Typographic technical: a focused developer CLI whose buying questions are "does it reproduce" and "does it fit my stack"; the recipe's Hero A (offset statement plus a working trace instrument with input, output and one failure state) matches the tape exactly.

No mono body paragraphs.
- Composition and grid: 1280px max content; hero 8/4 split for statement and install card, then the tape at full content width; sections use a two-column head (claim left, elaboration right) and a single-column body; prose kept to ~60ch while artifacts (tape, pins grid, code) widen.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: a cool off-white (#f4f5f2) with white panels and hairline rules, because the tool is read in a terminal next to a CI web page and the light ground keeps the only dark surface (the terminal example) meaning "this is the real console"; one blue accent for the action, the laptop track header and the playhead; red and green reserved for fail/pass meaning; depth from rules and one tint, no shadows except the phone menu popover.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the twin-track tape: seven recorded events (env, clock, random, GET, timer, POST, assertion) on a CI track; the laptop track fills in on Replay (380ms per event, stoppable), on scrubbing the step slider (0–7, keyboard arrows), and switches between "rerun replay ci-8841" (every event matches, failure reproduced) and "npm test (no rerun)" (six of seven differ, test passes). Default state is the completed replay. Outcome announced in a `role=status` live region. Labelled "Sample recording written for this page. - Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero (statement + install + tape) → "The test is not random. Its inputs are." Omitted on purpose: any social-proof section (logos, testimonials, star counts): nothing was supplied; the pricing section says so in one line beside the claim instead.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: pressing Replay or scrubbing the step slider; motion is limited to a 500ms background flash on the newest filled row (guides the eye to the playhead) and 150ms button fills; no entrance animations, nothing moves on its own for more than ~2.7s and it stops by itself.
