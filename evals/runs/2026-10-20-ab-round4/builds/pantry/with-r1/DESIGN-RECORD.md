## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: parents cooking weeknight dinners for picky kids, usually on a phone in the kitchen; a weeknight dinner planner (iPhone app, pre-launch); free now, a paid family plan with shared lists later at an unset price; no brand assets supplied, no users, reviews, press or partner recipes; the category is "grey recipe apps", so the page must be remembered after one visit.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): parents of picky kids / the week's dinners get planned without a big shop and without a fight at the table / the app starts from what is already in the fridge and cupboard, plans seven dinners around it, and lists only what is missing.
- Five-second line, quoted as shown in the first screen (who + what): "a free iPhone app for parents of picky kids" (the lead paragraph: "Pantrywise is a free iPhone app for parents of picky kids. Tick what's in your fridge and cupboard; it plans seven weeknight dinners around that and lists only what's missing.")
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: doing nothing (deciding at 5:40 pm from whatever is there); a recipe app such as Mealime that picks dinners first and then sends you shopping for everything; SuperCook-style "recipes from your ingredients" sites that answer tonight, not the week, and keep no shopping list or kid rules.
- The one advantage only this product has: it plans a whole week backwards from the shelf (perishables first), keeps each child's no-list as a rule that changes the plate rather than a ban, and produces a list of only the gap.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: existing noun "dinner planner" framed as "X for Y": a weeknight dinner planner for parents of picky kids; costs nothing to teach, the twist (fridge first, not store first) is carried by the headline.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: best fit is a parent who cooks most weeknights for one or more picky children and shops once a week on a phone; not for recipe browsers, batch-cook meal preppers, macro trackers, or anyone who wants partner recipes with photos and ratings.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet (the brief's own phrases "picky kids", "weeknights", "on a phone, often in the kitchen" and "only what's missing" were used; no interviews, tickets or reviews exist).
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: "what does joining cost me, and what do I get?" → beside the button: "Free to use. One email address, and we write when the app is ready. A paid family plan with shared lists comes later; its price isn't set."
- Conversion action and destination: "Join the iPhone waitlist" → https://pantrywise.example/waitlist (the client's URL, used as given; the page carries no form of its own).
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Will my kids actually eat any of this?" → the "The kids won't eat" veto chips inside the hero planner (dinners get skipped or re-plated live) and the Picky eaters section (two example no-lists and the egg-fried-rice → three-piles plate).
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "How much work is telling it what I have?" → the shelf itself: twelve tappable things, no amounts, and the "Tick what you have" step with the use-soon mark.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "Does it really shrink the shopping?" → the live "To buy" list on the sheet and the phone-sized list in "The shopping list is only what's missing", both computed from the shelf the visitor set.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: expressive world. A playful consumer product for parents, where a physical world (fridge, magnet, paper) can organise the page without a dashboard; functional entrance was the runner-up (input → output) and its principle is kept inside the hero artifact.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: display = a Bookman-style heavy serif stack (TeX Gyre Bonum here; Bookman Old Style, Charter, Georgia as fallbacks on visitors' machines) for h1/h2/h3, the day labels and the wordmark, because the register is a 1970s cookbook and a kitchen notice, not a SaaS grotesk; text = Inter with Avenir/Segoe/Roboto fallbacks for body, chips, lists and buttons, a utility sans that reads at 15–19 px on a phone. No web fonts are loaded (the page must open offline), so every face is a system stack and the record names the rendered one. Inter is used only as the body face, as the recipe allows.
- Composition and grid: 1260 px max width, 12-column grid: copy row 8/4, planner 6/6 (5/7 before the structural revision), section heads 7/5, aisle section 5/7, cost 1/1. Breakout: artifacts take the full content width while prose keeps 58ch. Phone: single column, shelf in two columns.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: yolk yellow (#FFCF4A) as the page ground because the brief's category is grey and the product lives in a kitchen (butter, yolk, the inside light of a fridge); ice (#EEF4F6) for the fridge interior; warm paper (#FFFCF3) for the sheet and notes; plum-black ink (#211B33) for text and the dark band. Accents by state: tomato (#D63A1C) for the action and for "buy" (what is missing), leaf green (#1E6A3C) for "uses" and the ticked mark. Material is paper, enamel and a magnet: outlines, one offset shadow under the sheets, no glows, no gradients.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: the sample planner. Twelve shelf chips (native checkboxes), three veto chips, a reset button; `planner.js` scores every dinner by what it uses from the shelf, perishables first, buying little; one tub of rice stretches to one dinner, a pack of chicken to two; "no mushrooms" and "nothing spicy" exclude dinners, "nothing mixed in" keeps the dinner and adds a "kids:" plating note; the sheet shows Mon–Sun with uses / buy / kids, the "To buy" list with counts, and a status line in a live region; the aisle section's phone list mirrors the same list. Empty shelf and too-few-fits states are explicit sentences, not blank rows.
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero with planner → It starts from your shelf, not from a recipe list (three real steps) → Picky eaters get a veto, not a lecture (dark band) → The shopping list is only what's missing (live list in a phone frame) → Free now. Family plan later (cost) → What parents ask before they join (four questions) → Start the week from your fridge (final action) → footer. Omitted on purpose: the proof slot (logos, quotes, ratings, store badges), because none exists; also no feature grid.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: tapping a shelf or veto chip re-plans the week; motion only confirms what changed: a 700 ms yellow opacity flash on rows whose dinner changed, a 240 ms scale pulse on the "To buy" count, 120 ms press scale on buttons and chips. Nothing moves by itself.
