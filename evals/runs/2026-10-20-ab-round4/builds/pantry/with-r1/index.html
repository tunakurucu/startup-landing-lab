It starts from your shelf, not from a recipe list.

Recipe apps pick the dinners first and send you shopping for everything. Pantrywise reads the shelf first, so most of the week is already paid for.

  1. Tick what you have.

    A short list of staples you tick once, plus whatever is actually in there tonight. Leftovers and open packets get a use soon mark, so they are cooked in the first days, not thrown out on Sunday.

Picky eaters get a veto, not a lecture.

Each kid gets a short no-list: a few things they won’t eat, and a rule or two. The plan treats a no-list as a rule, not a ban. It keeps the dinner and changes the plate.

The shopping list is only what’s missing.

Seven dinners from a recipe app can mean a list as long as your arm and a full trolley. Planned from the shelf, the same week comes down to the few things you don’t have.

Things are counted the way you buy them: a tin, a bag, a bottle. Something used twice in the week appears once, with a note. Big tick targets, because you’re holding a basket.

Later, the paid family plan will share one list between phones, so whoever is passing the shop sees what is still unticked.

What parents ask before they join.

Do I have to weigh or count things?

No. The shelf is a list of things, not amounts. “Half a bag of spinach” is as exact as it gets, and the plan stretches a tub of leftover rice to one dinner, a pack of chicken thighs to two.

What if the fridge is nearly empty?

Then the week leans on the cupboard and the list gets longer. Untick everything in the sample above and you’ll see what it does: with nothing on the shelf, it says so instead of inventing a week.

Where do the dinners come from?

From a list of plain weeknight dinners written for the app, not from a recipe site or a partner. The names on this page are examples of that list, not a promise of any particular dish.
