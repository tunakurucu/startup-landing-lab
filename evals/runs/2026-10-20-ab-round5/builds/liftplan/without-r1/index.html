What it replaces

A spreadsheet template copied from job to job, and load charts read off PDFs by hand.

Both work until they don't. The template carries last site's crane and last site's ground. The chart has three pages for the same boom and the hook block never gets deducted. The signed copy is a photo on someone's phone.

Liftplan holds your fleet's charts and your own plan template. Every lift starts from the chart, not from the last lift.

How a lift goes through

Four handovers. The plan is one record the whole way, not a file that gets re-sent.

  1. The lift is entered

    Load, rigging, crane and configuration, radius, ground. From the office or standing at the pick point.

    Site manager or appointed person

  2. Liftplan checks it

    Against the crane's chart, outrigger loads and the allowable bearing pressure, sling angles and accessory ratings. Anything outside limits is flagged in the plan.

    Liftplan

  3. The appointed person signs

    Reviews every figure, changes the method if they want to, writes their own conditions, and signs on the tablet. Or refuses the lift, and the record says so.

    Appointed person

  4. The lift team has the signed plan

    Crane supervisor and slinger see the current version on the day. A change on the day goes back to the appointed person, not onto a Post-it.

    Crane supervisor

What it checks

The plan follows the sections your appointed person already works through. These are the checks Liftplan runs in each one. Flagged items are shown in the plan with the limit they are measured against.

Lift plan sections Your template, your section order
1

LoadWeight, dimensions, centre of gravity

  • Gross load adds rigging, spreader beam and hook block to the stated weight
  • Stated weight is matched to the source: drawing, delivery note or weighed
  • Flags a load weight with no source given
2

Crane and configurationBoom length, counterweight, outrigger extension, slew range

  • Reads the chart for the exact configuration, not the best page in the PDF
  • Flags a configuration the crane supplier's chart does not cover
3

Radius and capacityPick radius, set-down radius, the worst of the two

  • Capacity at each radius, interpolated between chart rows
  • Utilisation against your own critical-lift threshold, 75% by default
  • Flags a lift above 100% of chart as not possible in this configuration
4

Ground and outriggersBearing pressure, mats, services and voids

  • Outrigger loads from the crane's tables, or a stated worst case if the tables are not loaded
  • Mat pressure against the allowable bearing pressure you give for that spot
  • Flags ground with no bearing figure, and asks for an investigation or a temporary works check
5

Lifting accessoriesSlings, shackles, spreader beams, lifting points

  • Working load limit of each accessory against its share of the load at the sling angle used
  • Flags accessories without a current inspection on your register
6

Method and exclusion zoneSequence, wind limit, communication, who stands where

  • Wind limit from the crane's chart and the load's surface area, written into the plan
  • Exclusion zone and tag lines written as conditions the crane supervisor confirms on the day
  • Flags a method the appointed person has not written in their own words
Every check shows its source, its limit and its figure. Nothing is hidden in a formula cell.

Liftplan doesn't decide whether a lift goes ahead. Your appointed person does.

A flag is not a verdict. The appointed person can accept a flagged lift with their own conditions, change the crane, or stop it. The plan records what they decided and why.

Liftplan does

  • Read the chart and the outrigger tables so nobody has to by torchlight
  • Put every figure next to its limit
  • Keep one current version of the plan
  • Record who signed, when, and what they changed

Liftplan does not

  • Approve a lift
  • Replace the appointed person's planning or the crane supervisor's control of the lift
  • Sign anything on anyone's behalf
  • Stand in for a temporary works check or a ground investigation
