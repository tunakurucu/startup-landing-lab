## Product interpretation
- Audience, category, business model, brand constraints: Adult children in England and Wales who have just become a parent's executor, mostly on a phone in the first weeks after a death; a checklist (consumer, pre-launch, no professional buyer); business model not stated in the brief; no brand files supplied beyond the name Settle; tone must be calm and kind, never selling to someone grieving; must say "not legal advice" visibly.
- Promise (who / what changes / how): An adult child settling a parent's affairs / knows who to tell, in what order, and what each one needs before they phone / a checklist that orders every account, bill and agency and carries the documents and letter each needs.
- Five-second line, quoted as shown in the first screen (who + what): "Settle is a checklist for adult children settling a parent’s affairs in England and Wales."
- Current alternative, including doing nothing: the solicitor's generic PDF, the GOV.UK "what to do when someone dies" steps, a notebook of phone numbers, or doing nothing and answering whichever letter arrives next.
- The one advantage only this product has: the list re-orders itself to what the parent actually had, and each line carries the documents and the letter that one organisation needs; the PDF is generic and the notebook has no order.
- Category frame (existing, subcategory or new) and what it costs to teach: existing noun, "a checklist", framed as "a checklist for adult children settling a parent's affairs"; costs nothing to teach.
- Best-fit customer, and who it is not for: an adult child acting as executor for a parent's estate in England and Wales, doing most calls themselves; not for Scotland or Northern Ireland, not for professional executors or solicitors, not for estates a firm is handling end to end.
- Users' own words (three phrases with source, or 'none yet'): none yet (the brief supplies the client's phrases "a notebook of phone numbers" and "hours on hold", which the page adapts; no user interviews or reviews exist).
- Biggest reason not to click, and the line that answers it: "Is this going to sell to me or ask for my details while I am grieving?" Answered beside the button: "Nothing to sign up for. It copies your list as plain text, to paste wherever you keep notes."
- Conversion action and destination: primary "Copy my checklist" copies the visitor's ordered list to the clipboard (local, real result; visible fallback text box if the clipboard refuses). Secondary interest form "Save my request here" saves the email in the browser only and says so; there is no destination yet (missing destination listed under To confirm).
- Visitor doubt 1 → artifact: "Does it know the order and what each one needs?" → the signature checklist itself, 11 ordered lines in the default example, each with "Have ready" tags and the letter marker.
- Visitor doubt 2 → artifact: "Does it fit my parent's situation?" → the eight "what they had" toggles that re-order the list, and the Tell Us Once and probate lines whose text changes with them.
- Visitor doubt 3 → artifact: "What do the letters actually say, and is this legal advice?" → the sample bank letter with fill-in fields, and the scope section plus the "not legal advice" line at the top of the artifact.

## Hypothesis
- Archetype and why: Workflow theater. The product's value is a sequence of steps with a visible artifact per step; one work item (the visitor's estate) moves through selectable states, and the recipe's "narrow explanation / wide recurring workspace" fits a checklist beside a short promise.
- Type roles, faces and why they fit this product: Display and voice: Lora 600 (SIL OFL), a book serif that reads like a letter from someone who has done this, not a form. Tool and data: Inter 400/500/600 for chips, tags, numbers and body; its tabular figures keep the line numbers steady. Two registers (the humane serif for what the page says, the neutral sans for what the tool does), anchored by one ink colour and one spacing scale. Inter is the everywhere face; it is used only as the text/UI face, with Lora on every heading, so TY-10 passes as recorded.
- Composition and grid: one 1240px container; hero 5/7 with a sticky lede; the kit of three documents at full width; letter section 5/7 reversed in weight (text left, sheet right); scope as two short ruled blocks; status as prose beside a bordered form. Density: open promise → dense list → medium documents → quiet letter → short scope → short status.
- Ground, colour and material, and why this ground: a pale cool green-grey ground (#eef1ee), white paper sheets with hairline rules and square corners, one accent (deep green #1d5a46) for the primary action, ticked state and the certified-copy tag, and a pale highlighter yellow only on lines that just changed. Chosen against the category default (warm cream, soft pastels, leaf illustration) and the anti-default (fintech black); the cool paper reads like a document on a table under daylight, calm without being funereal. No shadows, no gradients, no textures.
- Signature artifact and its local behaviour: `section#checklist[data-signature]`: eight checkbox chips ("what they had"), a counts strip (`role=status`), "Start again", and an ordered list rebuilt on every change (14 possible lines, 11 in the default example). Tell Us Once, the will line and the probate line change their text with the ticks; new or changed lines get a short highlight. "Copy my checklist" copies the current list as numbered plain text with "Have ready" items; if the clipboard refuses, a read-only text box appears beside the pressed button.
- Signature on a phone (where the result shows after a tap): chips sit in two columns; the counts strip and the top of the list are directly beneath them, so after a tap the new count and the first changed line are in view (checker MB-07: 10 controls used, nothing off-screen, nothing dead).
- Sample audit (counts, weekdays, states and stated rules agree): default example "11 to contact · 4 want a certified copy · 6 come with a letter" matches the rendered lines (certified copy: bank, mortgage, pension, probate; letters: bank, insurer, mortgage, pension, utilities, phone). Example label names owned home, bank, pension, State Pension, phone and a will, matching the ticked chips. Tell Us Once's "car itself is line N" resolves to the DVLA line number when a car is ticked. No dates with weekdays on the page. Singular forms ("1 wants") handled.
- To confirm with the client (claims and terms kept off the page): every factual line of the checklist (5-day registration limit, what Tell Us Once covers, bank bereavement practice, empty-home insurance terms, meter readings, Royal Mail requirements, when probate is needed) before launch; whether Settle drafts letters for exactly the lines marked "letter"; pricing or "free" (nothing stated, so nothing shown); a real destination for the interest form (none exists; the form saves in the browser only); an absolute URL for `og:image` once a domain exists; self-hosting Lora and Inter as woff2 (the page names installed faces and falls back to Georgia/Arial elsewhere); whether to add Citizens Advice or GOV.UK links (no URLs were supplied, so none were invented).
- Section sequence, with one section deliberately omitted: hero with the live checklist → what each line needs (three documents as sheets) → the letters (sample bank letter) → scope (England and Wales; not legal advice) → where it stands (not ready; interest saved locally) → footer. Omitted on purpose: a "how it works" three-step row and any proof/testimonial band (nothing real exists); a final repeated CTA block was built, then cut to shorten the phone page.
- Primary interaction and motion purpose: ticking a chip re-orders the list; the only motion is a 1.4s highlight fade on lines that changed (so the eye finds what moved) and a 150ms press scale on buttons. No entrances, no scroll motion.
