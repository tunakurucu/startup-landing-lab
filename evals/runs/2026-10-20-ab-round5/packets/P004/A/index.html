Who to tell, in what order, and what each one needs.

Settle is a checklist for adult children settling a parent’s affairs in England and Wales. It puts every account, bill and agency in order, with the documents and the letter each one needs.

Nothing to sign up for. It copies your list as plain text, to paste wherever you keep notes.

Tick what they had. The list puts itself in order.

Example: a parent who owned their home, with a bank account, a pension, the State Pension and a phone contract. Change it to match yours. A general order for England and Wales, not legal advice.

The letters are written; you add the account number

Each line marked letter in the checklist has one. They say the same few things, which is the point: the organisation gets what it needs first time, and you do not write the sentence about your parent’s death over and over.

  • What happened, and when.
  • Which account or policy, so it can be found.
  • What is enclosed, so nobody asks for it again.
  • What you are asking them to do.

This one is for a bank. The dotted parts are what you fill in.

Sample letter, written for this pageTo a bank or building society

To the Bereavement Team
name of the bank or building society

Dear Bereavement Team,

I am writing to tell you that my mother or father, their full name, died on date of death. They held an account with you, number account number.

I enclose a certified copy of the death certificate. I am their executor under their will.

Please could you:

  1. freeze the account and stop any payments going out of it;
  2. send me a statement of the balance on the date of death;
  3. tell me what you need from me to close the account, and whether you will pay the funeral director’s invoice from it.

My address and phone number are at the top of this letter.

Yours faithfully,
your full name

It covers England and Wales, and it is not legal advice

England and Wales only, for now

Scotland and Northern Ireland register deaths differently and settle estates under different rules. Settle does not cover them at launch, and the order on this page is not written for them.

A general order, not advice about your parent’s estate

The list is the order most estates follow. Yours may have a step this page does not know about. A solicitor can tell you what applies to you, and nothing here replaces that.
