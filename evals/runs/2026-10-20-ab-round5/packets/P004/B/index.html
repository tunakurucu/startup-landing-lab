Settle England and Wales

Everyone you need to tell, in the order to tell them.

After a parent dies, the paperwork arrives all at once. Settle is a checklist that takes it one contact at a time: which accounts, bills and agencies to get in touch with, what each one will ask you for, and a letter you can send to each.

It isn’t ready yet. If you’d like to know when it is, there’s a place to leave your email at the bottom of this page.

Settle is a checklist, not legal advice, and it only covers England and Wales. If something about your parent’s estate is unclear or disputed, a solicitor or Citizens Advice can help in a way a list can’t.

What Settle gives you for each one

Every contact on the list opens to a page like this: what they’ll ask for, what happens next, and a letter you can adapt and send.

The bank or building society

Takes about twenty minutes on the phone, or ten in a branch

What they’ll ask for

  • A death certificate (original or a certified copy)
  • Your own photo ID and proof of address
  • The account numbers, if you have them; they can look them up from a name and address if not

What happens next

The accounts are frozen the same day. Standing orders and direct debits stop, so anything paid from the account, from the gas to the TV licence, now needs telling. The bank will pay the funeral director’s invoice directly from the account if you send it to them. Each bank sets its own limit for releasing the balance without probate; they’ll tell you whether you need to apply.

One thing worth knowing

The Death Notification Service is free and tells most UK banks and building societies in one go. Settle sends you there first, and keeps the list of who’s been told.

The letter

Dear Bereavement Team,

I am writing to tell you that my mother, full name, died on date. I am her executor / next of kin. I enclose a certified copy of the death certificate. Please let me know what you need from me to close her accounts, and confirm whether a grant of probate will be required.

Yours faithfully,

What it is, and what it isn’t

  • It’s a list, in order. What has to happen this week, what can wait a month, and what only matters once probate comes through. Most of it can wait longer than you’d think.
  • It’s one page per contact. The phone number, the documents they’ll want, how long they tend to take, and a letter to send if you’d rather write than ring.
  • It isn’t legal advice. Settle tells you who to contact and what to bring. It doesn’t tell you what the will means, whether tax is due, or what to do if the family disagrees. For those, see a solicitor.
  • It isn’t the probate application. You apply for probate through GOV.UK. Settle tells you whether you’re likely to need it and what to gather first.
  • It’s England and Wales only, for now. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different rules, and we’d rather not guess at them.
