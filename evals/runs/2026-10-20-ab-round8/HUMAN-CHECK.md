# Human spot check, round 8

After the round was graded, the maintainer looked at the four primary pairs blind, as the graders' packets labelled them (A and B). He saw only still images of the first screen at 1440 and 390 and was asked which page he would ship. He was told which page used the skill only after answering.

| Brief | Picked | Skill page was | AI graders preferring the skill page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| contractcheck | A | B | 3 of 3 |
| staplepress | B | A | 2 of 3 |
| swaptide | B | A | 3 of 3 |
| tallyhall | A | B | 3 of 3 |

**He picked the page built without the skill in all four,** against 11 of 12 AI grader preferences the other way.

## His reasons, summarised

- **Contractcheck:** the skill page looked cluttered, with a title face and logo he disliked, a dull background, an ugly failed-state chip in the picture and a misaligned response body. The other page had good spacing, few words and a more modern picture.
- **Staplepress:** the skill page was cluttered, with many buttons and a picture that "doesn't tell me anything", so he didn't know what to do. The other page was cleaner, with less text and a well-placed picture whose small details looked good.
- **Swaptide:** he liked the other page's phone picture, its softness and its two simple buttons. The skill page opened with a table of information he didn't want to read, tall boxes, and small buttons that weren't well structured or proportioned.
- **Tallyhall:** the other page was clean and still interactive at the top, with a small phone picture and a clear trial button.

## What this means for the results

The AI graders operated each page and scored ten criteria. Six of those criteria (proof, interaction, honesty, mobile, fit and clarity) reward what the skill adds: a working demo in the first screen, every example labelled, every control answering. A person judging the first screen as a visitor would weighed visual calm, few words, one strong picture and type quality much more heavily. Graders from the same model family as the builder shared the builder's idea of a good page.

This one check doesn't measure visitor preference in general: it's one person, first screens only, four pairs. But it contradicts the AI result completely, so the round 3–8 scores above should be read as "preferred by AI graders on this rubric", not "looks better to people". The next version is judged first by people.
