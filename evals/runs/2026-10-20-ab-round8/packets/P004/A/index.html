How a Sunday works

The same four steps every week, whoever is on the rota.

  1. 1After the service

    Two people count

    Two volunteers count the offering together on one phone: notes, coins, envelopes and cheques, each on its own line.

  2. 2Before anyone goes home

    Both sign it off

    Each counter checks the total and signs. One signature isn’t enough, so no one is ever left vouching for the money alone.

  3. 3As soon as it’s signed

    It posts to the ledger

    The signed count goes into the books with both counters’ names on it. The treasurer sees it without retyping a thing.

  4. 4In January

    Statements are ready

    Each donor’s giving for the year is gathered into a statement, built from the counts you already signed.

Get January back

Without it, giving statements mean January evenings spent with last year’s counting sheets. With Tallyhall they come from the counts your volunteers signed each week, so the figures on the statement are the figures in the books.

  • One statement for each donor, for the whole year
  • Built from signed counts, not copied out again
  • In pounds for UK organisations, in dollars for US ones

It keeps the record. It never touches the money.

If you’re weighing up whether to trust software with the collection, start with what Tallyhall doesn’t do.

  • It isn’t a payment processor

    No card readers, no transfers, no money passing through us. Your cash and cheques reach the bank the way they do now.

  • It doesn’t take online donations

    There’s no giving page and no donate button. Tallyhall is for counting and recording what you’ve received.

  • It does keep two names on every count

    Every count in the ledger carries the signatures of the two people who counted it, which is the protection your volunteers and your donors are owed.

For the committee or trustees

The treasurer uses Tallyhall every week, but it’s usually a committee that decides. These are the questions they tend to ask.

What does it actually do?
It records the Sunday count on a phone, has two volunteers sign it off, posts it to the ledger, and produces each donor’s giving statement at year end.
Does it handle any money?
No. It isn’t a payment processor and doesn’t take online donations. It keeps the record; your bank keeps the money.
Who else uses it?
About 40 congregations. We don’t publish their names or their figures, and we won’t publish yours.
Does it work where we are?
Yes, if you’re a church or charity in the UK or the US.
What will it replace?
The paper counting sheet, the treasurer’s own spreadsheet, and writing giving statements by hand each January.
How do we try it?
Start a free 60-day trial. You can keep the old counting sheet going alongside it for a few Sundays and compare the two.
