For nurse managers

You see the swap before it happens, with the checks already done.

Swaptide checks each swap against the staffing rules you set for your unit before it reaches you. If something doesn't fit, you see which rule and why. Then you decide.

  • Skill mix

    Whether the shift still has the certifications and experience you need on it once the swap goes through.

  • Hours limits

    Weekly hours and rest between shifts for the nurse picking it up, against the limits you've configured.

  • One tap to decide

    Approve or decline from your phone or desk. Both nurses are told either way.

What Swaptide doesn't do

It handles swaps. The rest stays where it is.

Clear lines, so nobody on the unit has to wonder.

Decisions

It doesn't approve swaps.

Every swap goes to the unit manager, who approves or declines it. The checks are there to inform that decision, not replace it.

Agreements & policy

It doesn't change your rules.

Union agreements and hospital policy still apply as they do today. Swaptide checks swaps against limits your unit sets; it doesn't override them.

Pay

It doesn't handle payroll.

Pay, differentials and timekeeping stay in the systems you already use.

Questions

Common questions

Who has the final say on a swap?

The unit manager, every time. Swaptide checks the swap against your unit's rules and shows the result, but nothing is approved until the manager taps approve.

What happens when a swap breaks a rule?

It's flagged before the manager sees it, with the rule it breaks: for example, a shift that would drop below your skill mix. The manager still decides what to do with it.

Does it change anything in our union agreement or hospital policy?

No. Those apply exactly as they do now. Swaptide checks against the limits your unit configures; it doesn't set or override them.

Does it handle pay?

No. Swaptide doesn't handle payroll. Pay stays in the systems you already use.

What results has the pilot shown?

One medical-surgical unit has used it for three months. We don't have measured results we can share yet, and we'd rather say that than give you a number we can't stand behind.
