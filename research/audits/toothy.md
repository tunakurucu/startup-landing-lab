# Toothy AI

Inspected: 2026-10-19 · desktop-audit · workflow-theater

https://toothy.ai/

A dental revenue page pairs a plain financial promise with two changeable insurance-work examples and careful pricing caveats.

## Company evidence

YC: verified-yc; batch: W25.

- desktop: 1363 × 936: rendered hero, section scan and selected interaction
- tablet: not inspected
- mobile: not inspected

## Observations

### Hero

Left-aligned Stop letting insurance slow your revenue statement, three checkmarked benefits and demo/learn actions face a verification or billing process panel on the right.

### Typography

Large bold sans-serif display, calm gray supporting copy and restrained uppercase section labels. Exact families unverified.

### Visual System

White page with purple brand accents, faint warm/cool glows, sparse rounded UI panels and small data-state chips. The workflow diagram is more specific than stock medical imagery.

### Page Sequence

Hero and two workflow views → verification/billing/dashboard services → outcomes and reported practice results → PHI/security reassurance → two-mode pricing and scope caveats → FAQ → final demo/footer.

### Interaction

Clicked Billing in the hero, replacing the verification timeline with billing tasks and collection/aging tiles. Clicked Billing & Collections in pricing; the selected tab and tier descriptions changed. No clinic details or demo request were submitted.

### Accessibility

Pricing uses named tabs with selected states and FAQ uses disclosure buttons. The top hero mode buttons have names; keyboard, screen-reader and contrast review remain untested.

## Interpretation

- Show a concrete before-to-after work trace for a complex service.
- Keep plan scope and pricing variability next to the buying action.
- Place privacy and human handoff information within the buyer journey.

Useful for a healthcare operations buyer who needs workflow visibility, process accountability and a sales conversation.

Risk: The page reports time saved and collection ratios without independent verification here. Awesomic’s case study is the design vendor’s promotional account of an earlier redesign; the current site was reviewed separately.

## Limitations

- One cloud Chromium session at a 1363 × 936 desktop viewport on 2026-10-19; the homepage can change.
- Tablet, mobile, keyboard-only, screen-reader, color contrast and reduced-motion behavior were not tested.
- Exact font names, animation timing, implementation libraries and conversion effects were not established.
- A third-party design mention is a selection lead, not an independent conversion or product-quality endorsement.
- Awesomic documented a Toothy redesign; this is vendor-authored evidence of design attention, not an independent compliment.
