# Casebook: editorial-platform

## Vercel · editorial-platform

https://vercel.com · desktop-mobile-audit · **reference tier**

A two-word category claim beside the brand triangle, then three use-case chapters, each proven by a named customer's live product and one usage number.

Observed: Left: a two-line h1 'Agentic Infrastructure' above a black 'Deploy now' pill and an outlined 'Talk to sales' pill. Centre: a solid black triangle with a soft shadow, missing from the measurement's first-screen capture but present in later captures. Right: three short stacked lines saying who it is for. Wide empty margins above and below; a seven-logo customer row (Meta, Blackbox.ai, DoorDash, OpenAI, SpaceX, The Weather Company, Polymarket) closes the first screen. Hovering 'Products' opened a full-width menu of three columns (Agent Stack, Core Platform, Tools) in large type and greyed the page; a click straight after the hover left it closed. Captures 2s apart at the top and mid-page were identical. Customer artifacts appeared during slow scrolling but were blank after instant jumps; the full-page captures are blank from the Mintlify section down. At 390 the logo row moved on its own and the mono subline changed between captures. Under reduced motion the hero was unchanged and artifacts still appeared.

Copy: H1: 'Agentic Infrastructure'. No subhead paragraph; instead three lines: 'For coding agents', 'To ship apps and agents', 'Automated by agents'. Primary button 'Deploy now'; secondary 'Talk to sales'; the header adds 'Get a Demo', 'Log In' and 'Sign Up'. The h1 names the category and the lines name agents and the teams building them as the audience. Each use case carries one customer claim, e.g. 'Zapier serves over 100 million monthly website visits on Vercel.'

Transfer: Organise a broad platform by the jobs buyers hire it for, and back each job with one named customer, one number and a four-item feature list. Let customers' real products be the imagery; a strict monochrome keeps those screenshots reading as evidence rather than decoration. Replace the subhead paragraph with a short list of who the product is for, placed beside the headline.

Avoid/limit: The first screen carries no product evidence and the h1 is an abstract category label. The proof depends on artifacts that render only as they scroll into view, so full-page captures and fast scrolls show large empty areas. No code, pricing or deploy flow appears on the page; developers have to click through to evaluate.

Measured 2026-10-20: desktop h1 64px, weight 400, tracking -0.06em; body 16px; 4 sections; phone h1 48px.

On phones: The triangle moves above a centred 48px h1; the three audience lines collapse to one mono line that changed between captures; buttons stretch full width and stack; nav reduces to the triangle and a menu button; the logo row becomes a cropped strip that scrolls by itself. Case studies stack heading → artifact (overlapping cards cropped at the right edge) → stat → features. Page height measured 5557px against 5930px at 1440.

## Harvey · editorial-platform

https://www.harvey.ai · desktop-mobile-audit · **reference tier**

A serif declaration with a full-width product dashboard directly beneath, then a buyer split, an auto-advancing product accordion and a stack of enterprise proof.

Observed: Split first screen under a black funding announcement bar: a two-line serif h1 on the left; on the right a one-sentence trust claim and a square black 'Request a Demo' button. Directly below, a full-width rounded panel with a dark painterly texture frames a large product screenshot, 'Whitford Lane's Command Center', with usage charts. The nav repeats 'Request a Demo' beside 'Login'. The Product Innovation list auto-advances, with a growing progress line and a 'Pause auto-advance' button; it had reached Spaces on arrival. Clicking 'Vault' set aria-expanded to true, expanded its description and 'Learn More', collapsed Spaces, and swapped the right panel from a 'Project Titan — Acme Co' space to a 'Create a vault' file-source dialog. Product images were semi-transparent in some scroll captures and blank after instant jumps. With reduced motion the progress line did not change across captures 2s apart; in the normal run it did.

Copy: H1: 'Build a Frontier Legal Organization'. Subhead: the world's top law firms and in-house legal teams trust Harvey with their highest-stakes work. Primary button 'Request a Demo', repeated in the nav, hero, Harvey II block and closing band; no trial or pricing path appears. Audiences are named explicitly (law firms, in-house teams), and scale is asserted with numbers (3,500+ organizations, 200,000+ professionals, 80+ AmLaw 100 firms) and the funding headline.

Transfer: Put a real, detailed product screen at full width directly under the headline so the first scroll lands on product rather than marketing. Split readers by buyer type early (law firm or in-house) before the feature tour begins. If a feature list auto-advances, sync it to a product panel, show progress, and give it a pause control. Use the serif for claims and numbers and the sans for explanation and UI, so voice and evidence stay visually distinct.

Avoid/limit: Much of the persuasion is accumulated enterprise proof (funding, logos, AmLaw counts, certifications) that a young company cannot copy. A single demo-request path excludes evaluators who want to try the product. Dense dashboards become illegible at 390, and the page runs to 14,611px on a phone.

Measured 2026-10-20: desktop h1 72px, weight 400, tracking -0.012em; body 14px; 9 sections; phone h1 48px.

On phones: The announcement becomes a scrolling ticker; nav reduces to the wordmark and a menu button that opens a black full-screen overlay with large serif items and 'Request a Demo' / 'Login' buttons. The h1 wraps to three lines at 48px above the subhead, button and a shrunken dashboard. Logos form three columns; buyer cards stack; the accordion becomes every item expanded with its image; client videos stack as dark tiles showing an 'H' placeholder. Page height grows to 14,611px.

## Figma · editorial-platform

https://www.figma.com · desktop-mobile-audit · **reference tier**

The hero sets a four-word h1, a stacked collage of finished design work and one oversized black button side by side, letting outputs stand in for the tool.

Observed: Three zones on a white first screen: the h1 set left over three lines, a centred collage of overlapping artifacts (a phone audio-player UI with a bird photo, an orange chat card, photos), and a large black 'Get started' button on the right. The nav carries 'Log in', 'Contact sales' and a black 'Get started for free'. A pale-blue Config banner with its own button pins to the viewport bottom a few seconds after load. Clicked the template carousel's 'Next slide' arrow: the counter changed from '1 of 10' to '2 of 10' and the card row shifted one card left. The hero collage held still across ~7 s; the Config banner appeared at the viewport bottom; the logo marquee scrolled sideways, with a pause button at its end. Video tiles showed a 'Player error' overlay in this Chromium. At one scroll step the page re-flowed and the scroll position jumped back ~740px. Reduced motion: frames still, no animations reported running.

Copy: h1: 'The full-stack creative canvas'. Section lines name the audience as teams working with agents ('Teammates and AI agents work in the same space with shared context'; 'Made so your whole team can go from WIP to ship, together'). Primary buttons: 'Get started' (hero, closing bar) and 'Get started for free' (nav). Proof is one statistic, '95% of the Fortune 500 uses Figma', beside one named quote.

Transfer: Show what customers make with the tool rather than the tool's chrome when the output is more persuasive than the interface. Use one typeface and carry hierarchy with size and a black/grey value split, so a restrained page frames colourful media. Give the one action that matters unusual physical scale—a hero button rivalling the h1, a full-width closing bar. Pair one strong statistic with one named quote instead of a wall of testimonials.

Avoid/limit: The argument depends on video tiles: when playback fails the page shows error overlays and blank slots. The h1 is abstract and does not tell a newcomer what the product does. A persistent promotional banner competes with the primary button and covers content on both widths.

Measured 2026-10-20: desktop h1 56px, weight 400, tracking -0.022em; body 30px; 8 sections; phone h1 36px.

On phones: Nav keeps the logo, a black 'Get started for free' button and a menu icon. The hero stacks: h1 (36px) → artifact collage at reduced width → centred 'Get started'. The Config banner becomes a stacked block with a full-width 'Read more'. The media grid drops to two staggered columns; quote, stat and template sections stack; footer link groups run as one long list.

## ElevenLabs · editorial-platform

https://elevenlabs.io · desktop-mobile-audit · **reference tier**

A quiet split hero hands the first screen to a tabbed sampler of playable voice orbs, and three product families (Creative, Agents, API) organize the whole page.

Observed: A slim announcement bar ('Meet Eleven v4, our most emotive model yet.' plus Discover) sits above a non-sticky nav. The hero splits: two-line h1 with black 'Sign up' and outlined 'Contact sales' pills on the left, a three-line subhead on the right. Below, a wide rounded grey panel with three tabs (ElevenCreative, ElevenAgents, ElevenAPI) holds a carousel of gradient voice orbs with play buttons, captions (Characters, Narration, Conversational), prev/next arrows and a row of product sub-tabs that falls below the fold. Operated: the ElevenAgents hero tab. In four attempts it took a grey hover fill and one run reported aria-selected=true, but the panel still showed the ElevenCreative orb carousel up to 10 s later; no content swap was seen. Two first-screen captures 1.5 s apart were identical and no running CSS/WAAPI animations were reported at 1440. A 'Voice chat' floating button appeared bottom-right after load. Reduced motion: top and mid-page captures were static; the measurement records a reduced-motion rule.

Copy: H1: 'Bringing technology to life'. The subhead names three audiences (enterprises, creators, developers) and maps them to ElevenAgents (customer experience), ElevenCreative (content creation) and 'the leading AI voice generator'. Primary button: 'Sign up' (secondary 'Contact sales'). Section heads are plain capability statements ('Deploy agents that talk, type, and take action'); the closing band names the category 'AI Communication Platform' with 'Talk to sales' and 'Create an AI agent'.

Transfer: When one company sells distinct product families to different buyers, put a family switcher inside the hero so each audience finds its path before the long scroll. For a product whose output can be sampled, make samples (here, playable voice previews) the first-screen artifact instead of a feature list. Mirror the business structure everywhere: the same three families should organize hero tabs, section order, eyebrow labels and footer columns. Keep display type light and quiet when imagery supplies the colour and emotion, so the two do not compete.

Avoid/limit: The hero sampler is heavy: in a loaded headless session screenshots timed out and the tab switch never visibly completed, so the first interaction may feel unresponsive on weak devices. Grey secondary text is faint. The page runs about 10,800px at 1440 and 15,800px at 390, with much content behind tabs that scanners will not open. The light 48px h1 is easily outweighed by the sampler below it.

Measured 2026-10-20: desktop h1 48px, weight 300, tracking -0.02em; body 15px; 0 sections; phone h1 36px.

On phones: The nav collapses to 'Sign up' plus a menu; the announcement shortens to 'Introducing Eleven v4'. The hero stacks h1 (36px), subhead, then side-by-side buttons. Tabs shorten to Creative/Agents/API and the product sub-tabs move above the orbs as a sideways-scrolling pill strip, with one large orb and partial neighbours. The logo wall becomes two columns; app screenshots stack under their labels; bento cards go single-column; code samples keep their width and clip.
