# Make the promise inspectable

Assign one artifact to each main visitor doubt. Use an actual workflow rather than three abstract claims like fast, powerful and seamless. Write the words from the positioning inputs in [copy and positioning](copy-and-positioning.md); [page structure](page-structure.md) says where each piece goes.

Technical example: input event → retry policy → execution trace → final status. Show error and recovery, not only the happy path. If illustrative, use reserved .example domains and label sample data. Do not claim real measured latency.

Consumer example: starting material → playful choice → visible creation. The visitor should understand what they made and how to restart. Keep the activity optional and the main action obvious.

B2B example: unclear handoff → explicit owner and decision → review history. Trust comes from explaining control and limitations. Real customer logos and quotes help when the client supplies them and visitors would recognize them; invented ones never do.

When real proof exists, show it early: within the first two screenfuls or right beside the claim it supports (ST-04). Recognizable names, and quotes with a name, role, company and photo, are remembered far better than obscure logos or anonymous quotes, and visitors already discount on-site testimonials, so attribute every one. Use only proof that was supplied or verified. Never invent a logo, quote, metric or customer count, and never round one up (HP-01, HP-02). With no proof yet, the working product, the mechanism, the founders and honest scope carry the page.

At each section ask what the visitor now knows that they did not know above. Delete sections that repeat the same claim. Honesty sits beside the claim it qualifies: a label on the sample, a caption under the number, a line beside the button (HP-03). Never put qualifications in a separate section about what the company lacks or has not decided.
