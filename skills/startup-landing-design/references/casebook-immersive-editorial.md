## Mercury · immersive-editorial

https://mercury.com/ · partial-desktop

A cinematic opening connects business finance to the world the founder wants to build.

Observed: Centered statement and signup/demo actions sit over a landscape with a desk in the foreground. A financial-service disclaimer is visible at the bottom. Opened Products. A large multicolumn menu exposed banking, cards, payments and intelligence categories. No account application or demo transaction was submitted.

Transfer: Pair an aspirational opening with concrete product explanation. Keep material product qualifications close to the promise. Give a broad offer a navigable category structure.

Avoid/limit: Foreground text competes with the landscape. Navigation overlay obscured some lower-page review; full-page and responsive inspection still needed.

Measured 2026-10-19: desktop h1 49.3px, weight 480, tracking 0em; body 12px; 13 sections; phone h1 28px.

## Atomarine · immersive-editorial

https://atomarine.co · desktop-mobile-audit

A full-bleed render of floating platforms with a full-width uppercase claim along the bottom edge makes an unbuilt industrial system feel concrete.

Observed: A full-bleed aerial image of floating data-center platforms and a service vessel at sea fills the screen. The uppercase h1 'NUCLEAR DATA CENTERS AT SEA' runs the full content width along the bottom edge, with a yellow-dot label above it and a yellow 'SCROLL TO SEE THE PLATFORM ↓' cue at right. A three-line tagline sits mid-left. The nav holds only the wordmark and a yellow Contact button. Dragged the 'Gas today, nuclear tomorrow' comparison handle left: the divider moved from about 60% to about 15% of the width, so the branded marine-reactor vessel filled most of the frame over the LNG carrier. Section content appeared only after it entered view; captures taken under 0.5s after a scroll step showed blank sections. In the scale section, line drawings and a statement slid horizontally as I scrolled. Under reduced motion the hero video element still reported playback (about 1.5s elapsed).

Transfer: When the product is physical and not yet built, lead with a render that shows its scale in context and state the category in the headline. Order the narrative as constraint, answer, scale path, then transition story so a sceptical buyer meets the problem before the claim. Use a before/after slider when the pitch is a substitution, so the visitor performs the change themselves. Restrict accent colour to one hue for actions and labels so it survives across dark, white and photographic sections.

Avoid/limit: Reveal-on-scroll content leaves sections blank to fast scrollers, screenshots and possibly assistive tools. The page shows renders and claims (4X faster, <1.1 PUE) without sourcing on the homepage. A single Contact action and 14px body text limit depth for technical buyers, and the hero video kept playing under reduced motion.

Measured 2026-10-19: desktop h1 80.7px, weight 500, tracking 0em; body 14px; 8 sections; phone h1 42px.

On phones: The hero image is recropped to a portrait slice; the h1 drops to 42px and wraps to two lines under the label, losing the full-width edge effect, and the 'scroll to see' cue is gone. Stats become a single column; the constraint list moves above its photo; the four benefits stack under the platform image. Nav keeps wordmark and Contact. In my live 390 scroll most sections stayed blank in captures, while the batch full-page capture shows them.

## Juno · immersive-editorial

https://junocompanion.com/ · desktop-mobile-audit

A warm portrait photograph in an inset hero card, then photo-led step cards with chat overlays, frame a health tool as personal support rather than software.

Observed: An inset rounded card fills the first screen with a golden-hour photograph of a woman seated on a hillside. A translucent pill nav inside the card holds the logo, five links, 'Example report' and a white 'Download' pill. Bottom-left, a three-line h1, a two-line subline, a white 'Download Juno' pill and an 'HSA/FSA eligible' check mark sit over the photo. Not operated: both live visits returned HTTP 502 ('upstream request failed'), so no control, scroll or reduced-motion load was performed. The measurement record lists 3 running animations on load and 14 after scroll at 1440, and marks a reduced-motion rule as observed. In the batch desktop capture, a band of roughly 1,700px below the hero rendered blank.

Transfer: For a sensitive consumer category, lead with a human photograph and a plain-language claim before any interface. Show the product through what a user would say ('My headache is bad today') overlaid on real-life scenes rather than through screenshots. When the product handles health data, place clinical credibility (advisors, institutions) high on the page and data commitments beside the plan details. Keep eligibility details such as HSA/FSA directly under the primary button where purchase anxiety is highest.

Avoid/limit: The long photographic page depends on lazy-loaded images and reveals that rendered blank, grey or as alt text in captures. Stock-feeling portraits risk reading as generic. The desktop page is very long (14,619px), and the product UI itself is barely shown.

Measured 2026-10-19: desktop h1 64px, weight 500, tracking -0.03em; body 20px; 9 sections; phone h1 40px.

On phones: Nav collapses to logo and a menu button inside the pill. The hero photo is recropped so the face fills the top half, with text and CTA stacked beneath. Logos shrink to one row. How it works becomes numbered headings each followed by its image and bullets. Advisors form a compact two-column list; community videos scroll horizontally. Page height falls from 14,619px to 7,924px and measured sections from 9 to 8.

## Applied Electrodynamics · immersive-editorial

https://www.ae-dyn.com/ · desktop-mobile-audit

A long scroll-scrubbed canvas sequence of the camera and walls being 'seen through' replaces conventional feature sections entirely.

Observed: Near-black page with a rounded hero card holding a dark product render of a box-shaped camera with a striped-ring logo on its face; "A new kind of camera" sits bottom left inside the card. Below: a grey two-line subline ("See through walls, and much more."), a green "Preorder Now" pill and a centred down arrow. Header has the striped-ring wordmark left and "Backed by Y Combinator" plus green "Preorder" pills right. Operated the header "Preorder" link: the page jumped to the preorder form (#waitlist-head). On load only the down arrow pulsed. Scrolling scrubbed the canvas: the camera body turned and the wall scenes and captions changed with scroll position. With reduced motion the arrow kept pulsing and the scroll-scrubbed sequence still moved with scroll; the measurement record lists no reduced-motion rule.

Transfer: For hardware with a hard-to-believe capability, show the capability happening on a recognisable surface before any claims. Use one family at one weight and let white versus grey within a sentence do the hierarchy work. Keep a single accent colour reserved for the purchase action so it is findable at every scroll depth. If the story lives in a scroll-scrubbed canvas, provide a static fallback for reduced motion and for crawlers or captures.

Avoid/limit: Most of the page's information is locked in a canvas that renders blank in static capture and may load slowly; the claims list is three captions long. Reduced motion is not honoured. Credibility rests on logo tiles and a patent count rather than technical detail or images from the device itself that are labelled as such.

Measured 2026-10-19: desktop h1 83.8px, weight 500, tracking -0.025em; body 30px; 3 sections; phone h1 32px.

On phones: The two header pills stack, with "Backed by" shrinking to the word plus the Y mark. The hero card becomes a tall portrait panel with the h1 at 32px; body drops to 16px. The canvas sequence remains (hero region measures 5,317px). The logo grid becomes two columns, the form stacks under its heading, and footer pills form two columns.

## Clair Health · immersive-editorial

https://www.wearclair.com · desktop-mobile-audit

A soft-luxury editorial page: high-contrast serif headlines, full-bleed product renders and lifestyle video, with a floating pre-order pill throughout.

Observed: After a brief grey-green loading screen with a progress bar, a mauve-to-blush gradient shows a centred two-line serif headline "The first continuous hormone monitor" above a large render of a pearl-and-rose-gold wrist band cropped at the bottom. A frosted pill nav (About, FAQ, Partners, Careers, Ask Clair, white "Pre-order now") floats at top with a faint funding announcement link below; a cream "Pre-order now" pill sits bottom centre. Clicked the "About & Privacy" FAQ tab: the questions swapped to that set with "Is Clair a medical device?" open. On load a loader ran, then the hero video elements were reported playing. During scroll the life-stage list highlight moved down and the side panel changed colour and message without a click. With reduced motion the hero videos stayed paused and no load animations were reported, but the life-stage list still advanced during scroll.

Transfer: Pair a classical serif display with a neutral sans body to make a medical-adjacent device read as personal and premium rather than clinical. Translate abstract sensing into concrete daily messages ("Hot flashes likely. Stay cool tonight.") shown in context. Stack credibility in escalating order: data scale, named clinicians, then a real person on video. A persistent purchase pill works only if it never covers the copy it floats over; reserve space for it on mobile.

Avoid/limit: Long page (about 11,300px) with a loader before the hero adds delay. Several low-contrast greys carry real information. The floating pill covers text on mobile, and the scroll-driven list ignores reduced motion. The medical-device answer sits in the second FAQ tab near the bottom of the page.

On phones: Nav becomes logo plus hamburger; the announcement wraps to two lines; the headline sets in two lines at 48.8px; the band render is cropped off both edges. Scientist cards become a one-card carousel with dots, the FAQ tabs compress to small two-line labels, and the offer list and form stack. The floating pre-order pill remains on every screen.

https://buttoncomputer.com · desktop-mobile-audit

A full-bleed lifestyle video of the device clipped to a person opens a commerce-template page that ends in a buy box and FAQ.

## General Legal · immersive-editorial

https://general.legal · desktop-mobile-audit

A dark full-bleed photograph of a contract on a desk sets a premium law-firm mood, then the page alternates warm photography with dense proof, people and prices.

Observed: Full-bleed dark photograph of a 'Master Services Agreement' document and pen on a wooden table fills the first screen. Cream text sits bottom-left: a two-line headline, a two-line description ending 'billing for outcomes, not hours', and a translucent rounded panel splitting two paths: 'Meet with our team...' with 'Book a call' and 'Open a matter to get a flat-fee quote' with 'Start a matter'. A transparent nav overlays the photo with a cream 'Get Started' pill. Operated FAQ item '4. Transfer: Split the hero CTA into two labelled paths ('talk to us' vs 'start now with a fixed quote') when buyers arrive at different levels of readiness. Use a still-life of the actual work product (a contract) as hero imagery to signal the service without stock-photo people. Put real prices on the homepage when the business model's differentiator is pricing; it turns a positioning claim into something checkable. Show the named experts with credentials early when trust in a new kind of firm depends on who does the work.

## Known · immersive-editorial

https://knowndating.com · desktop-mobile-audit

The homepage is a single centered manifesto on black, a short protest against swiping, ending in one Apply to Join button.

Observed: knowndating.com redirects to known.com. The first screen is pure black with a wordmark top-left, three text links and an outlined Apply to Join pill top-right, and a narrow centered block of short sentences in four stanzas ('Profiles told us nothing...', 'Not a game. Not a job. Not a gamble.'). There is no headline, image or product shot above the fold. At 390 I operated the hamburger: it became an X and a black panel dropped down with three large links (How it Works, Guidelines, Journal) over the top of the manifesto. With motion on, manifesto lines changed between captures and the 390 full capture shows lines fading in from a blur. In motion mode only three press logos showed, the first cropped at the edge; under reduced motion all lines rendered at once, eight press logos appeared in a static grid, and paired captures were identical.

Transfer: When the product's pitch is a rejection of category norms, a short manifesto can carry the whole first screen without any product imagery. Pair a single-action page with one CTA repeated in the nav and at the end of the text, so the reader never has to search for the next step. Swap a desktop QR code for a direct install link on mobile so the download path fits the device.

Avoid/limit: The page explains almost nothing: no screenshot, no how-it-works, no pricing, and the real h1 is hidden from sight. The line-by-line blur reveal delays reading on load, and the low-contrast grey details may fail contrast checks.

Measured 2026-10-19: desktop h1 16px, weight 500, tracking 0em; body 16px; 1 sections; phone h1 16px.

On phones: Nav links collapse into a hamburger and the Apply to Join pill moves beside it. Stanzas rewrap to the same centered column. The footer QR code is replaced by an app icon labeled 'Tap to Install', and link columns go two-up then stack. Page grows to 1601px.
