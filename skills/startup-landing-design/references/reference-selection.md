# Select for the decision, not the industry label

Match references to visitor doubts. A developer asks what goes in, what comes out, and whether integration is credible. A consumer asks what experience becomes possible and how much effort it takes. An operations buyer asks what changes in the workflow and how they stay in control.

Use a three-row decision record:

| Role | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Product explanation | Observed artifact → visitor doubt answered → original artifact to build |
| Brand composition | Observed hierarchy → product fit → original composition |
| Counterexample | Attractive choice → why inappropriate here → constraint to retain |

Examples: transfer Resend's integration-before-feature-depth principle without its serif-on-black treatment. Transfer PostHog's navigable mental model without desktop icons or hedgehogs. Transfer Mercury's connection between ambition and product without its landscape, bank claims or signup form.

Avoid selection by fame, funding or a badge. YC membership verifies cohort metadata, not design quality. The casebook holds 52 dated records: 32 from October 19, 2026 were rendered at desktop and phone width, operated and measured; 20 older ones are desktop observations. Where a record says something was not verified, do not fill the gap with a guess. The casebook and the measured set are different things: the 52 casebook records were inspected and operated by hand, while a larger set of 211 homepages was measured by script for numbers only (type sizes, measure, sections, ground). The recipes' **Observed** lines quote the measured set; each recipe's **Observed in this family** line quotes only that family's casebook records.

The installed skill is self-contained through casebook.md and needs neither the gallery nor the network. If you also have this skill's source repository (startup-landing-lab), `research/sites.json` holds the full records; nothing there is required.
