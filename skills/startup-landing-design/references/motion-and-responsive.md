# Motion and responsive behavior

These timings are **recommended defaults**, not measurements of referenced sites. Animate state and orientation, never the availability of essential copy. Prefer transform and opacity; avoid layout-changing width/height animation. A spring-like curve can suit a playful brand but should not make a technical or sober tool feel bouncy. For what each motion pattern is for, and for scroll-driven, pinned, scrubbed and parallax motion, read [motion patterns](motion-patterns.md).

| Trigger | State change and purpose | Static alternative | Recommended duration/easing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Press a scenario tab | Replace trace and explanation to compare outcomes | Immediate selected state and content | 150–220ms opacity ease-out |
| Advance a workflow | Current step and connector update to explain order | Numbered list and selected step | 180–280ms transform/opacity ease-out |
| Submit safe local input | Result/status appears to confirm action | Immediate text update | 150–250ms opacity ease-out |
| First appearance of nonessential scene | Guide attention to the artifact once | Fully visible scene at first paint | 250–400ms opacity/translate ease-out |
| Playful user deal | Cards arrive as a response to an explicit choice | Final cards and live status immediately | 220–360ms each, 30–80ms stagger; overshoot only in playful families |

Micro-interactions usually belong at 150–250ms; entrances 250–400ms, Material 3's medium range. Duration grows with distance, controls pressed again and again sit at the short end, and no response to input runs past 400ms (recommended). Use one strong ease-out for entrances and in-place swaps, for example `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)`. Anything leaving the screen uses an accelerate curve such as `cubic-bezier(.3,0,.8,.15)` ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration/tokens-specs)) and runs shorter than its entrance (recommended: about 20%). Never ease-in an entrance, never start from `scale(0)` (start near .9 or from a small translate), and keep staggers at 30–80ms so a sequence never blocks input (MO-07). Every animation can be interrupted: a second click picks up from the current value, so prefer transitions to chained keyframes. Overshoot curves belong to playful families only. Name transitioned properties; `transition: all` fails the checker (MO-03). Put hover-only motion behind `@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` so touch screens never get stuck hover states, and keep the hovered element's hit area still by moving an inner child instead. Text a visitor must read is visible at first paint; new text swaps in or moves into place at full opacity, because a contrast checker can catch it half-transparent.

**Two scales.** The numbers above are for responses to input. One-time reveals of a section or the signature moment run longer: 700–900ms on a strong expo-out such as `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`, a 16–32px rise, 60–90ms stagger in reading order, fired once when the block is 85–90% into the viewport, with the page settled within 1.2 s of load and no preloader (Taste Skill v2.1 measured these on 99 recent Awwwards Sites of the Day). Text in a reveal moves at nearly full opacity (start at 0.6 or above) so it is never hidden; images and artifact parts may fade from 0. Use one signature moment on the page, not the same fade-up everywhere. Build scroll-driven reveals as progressive enhancement: the finished state is the default, and the motion sits inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` and `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`, with IntersectionObserver for one-shot triggers elsewhere.

**Every control answers.** A press scales to about 0.96 over 150ms, a result appears within 100ms of the input and in view, hover styles sit behind `(hover:hover)`, and motion is never the only feedback: a text change or a state label carries it too (Jakub Krehel, better-ui; Vercel interface guidelines).

## Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, nothing translates, scales, rotates, zooms, parallaxes or smooth-scrolls, and every state change and all content stay visible (MO-01). Short fades may stay: [WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) does not count opacity or color changes as motion, and [Apple's HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) replaces movement with fades under Reduce Motion. Keep them on containers, highlights and decorative layers, at 200ms or less (recommended); text swaps. Write motion inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` so the default is still, and set `scroll-behavior: smooth` only there.

## Things that move by themselves

Anything that starts on its own and moves, blinks, scrolls or updates for more than 5 seconds beside other content either stops within 5 seconds or has a visible, named pause control ([WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html), Level A; MO-05). That covers marquees, looping hero video, rotating carousels, animated backgrounds, ticking counters and pulsing dots. The reduced-motion query is a courtesy on top, not the control. The h1 and primary action never change by themselves. Prefer static sections to a carousel; one that stays changes only on request, stops after any interaction, never auto-rotates on phones, and repeats its content elsewhere ([page structure](page-structure.md)). Video never autoplays with sound.

## Phones, zoom and reflow

At 390px, replace wide artifacts with readable vertical steps and retain important labels. At 768px, test the awkward middle rather than retaining a cramped three-column desktop grid. Reserve media dimensions.

- **Targets.** Every pointer target outside running text is at least 24×24 CSS px, or its 24px circle touches no other target; links inside sentences are exempt ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), MB-03). On phones, the primary action, nav links and menu button are 44×44 (Apple HIG, MB-02); enlarge the hit area with padding or a pseudo-element rather than the visible box. Inputs use at least 16px text (MB-04).
- **Reflow at 320 CSS px** ([WCAG 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)): no page-level horizontal scroll, and nothing clipped, hidden or overlapping. Code and short tables may scroll inside their own labelled region that can take keyboard focus. At 320×256, sticky and fixed bars turn static or together take under 30% of the height (recommended).
- **Text spacing** ([WCAG 1.4.12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html), AX-11): with line-height 1.5, paragraph spacing 2em, letter-spacing 0.12em and word-spacing 0.16em applied, nothing is clipped or overlaps. Text containers grow with their text: no fixed heights and no `overflow: hidden` on labels or buttons.
- **Text resize** ([WCAG 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)): rem sizes and a rem term in every clamp let text reach 200%. Never disable zoom in the viewport meta (MB-01). Body text is at least 16px on phones (TY-05).
- **Viewport height.** Give the hero `min-height: 100svh` or size it by content; `svh` stays put while `dvh` resizes as the toolbar moves ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/viewport-units)). Never fix the height of a box that holds text.
- **Safe areas.** If you set `viewport-fit=cover`, pad fixed bars and edge-to-edge controls with `env(safe-area-inset-*)`; without it the browser insets the page itself ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/7929/designing-websites-for-iphone-x/)).
